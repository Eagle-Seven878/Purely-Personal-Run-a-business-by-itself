# Signature Story Options · For Analysis
**These are drafts built from your voice note, not facts.** Anything in [brackets] is a detail only you know. Fill in or replace with what actually happened, then choose one, or blend both, for the 2-minute opening in the run-of-show.

## What the voice note told us
Someone observed that Gilbert's strength is **orchestrating**: he activates the people at Norte Street, and the other leaders in the Alpha chapter, to carry cash-generating activities rather than doing everything himself. That is the live proof of all four topics.

---

## Option A · Norte Street (the team you activate)
**Angle:** Activating Potential → Ownership
**Length:** ~2 min

> "A while ago at Norte Street, I could have run every cash-generating activity myself. It would've been faster. Instead, I [asked X what they thought we should do / handed Y the outcome and let them decide how]. The first result wasn't perfect. But [what changed: they took initiative, brought a new idea, the activity grew to ___]. I realised the cash didn't come from me working harder. It came from activating the people around me."

**Fill in:** Who was activated? What did you ask or hand back? What was the result (people, pesos, or momentum)?
**Teaching link:** Believe out loud → Hand-back Script.
**Strength:** Concrete, shows ownership working in a real team.
**Risk:** Needs a real number or outcome to be credible.

---

## Option B · Alpha Chapter Leaders (leading leaders)
**Angle:** Developing, Not Taking Over → Questioning
**Length:** ~2 min

> "In the Alpha chapter, the other leaders are strong. My instinct is to step in and fix. What works better is asking: 'What would you do?' and 'What do you need from me?' When [leader name or "one leader"] [situation], I asked instead of told. They [action], and [result]. That's the difference between managing people and orchestrating them."

**Fill in:** What was the situation? What question did you ask? What did they do that you wouldn't have thought of?
**Teaching link:** Takeover Trap → Powerful Questions.
**Strength:** Speaks to peer leadership, the hardest case, and fits a mixed audience.
**Risk:** Avoid naming anyone without their permission.

---

## How to choose
| Criterion | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Fits Block 1 opener (activating potential) | Strong | Medium |
| Shows the "tell → ask" shift | Medium | Strong |
| Easy for a mixed audience to relate to | Strong | Medium |
| Needs the least permission or sensitivity | Strong | Medium |

**Recommendation:** Open with Option A (it sets up the whole session), and reference Option B briefly in Block 3 as the "even strong leaders get the urge to take over" example.

## Questions to sharpen either story
1. What exactly were the "cash-generating activities"? (name them)
2. What was the single question or sentence that unlocked someone?
3. What did you want to do instead, and what did it cost you to hold back?
4. What measurable result followed?
