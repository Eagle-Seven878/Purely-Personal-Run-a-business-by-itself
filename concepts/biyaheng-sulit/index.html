The fees that ruin budgets

What first-timers forget to count

These rarely show up on a booking site's total. Check each one for your destination.

Terminal fees at ports and some airports

Often cash only and paid on the day. Keep small bills ready.

Environmental and eco-tourism fees

Many islands and protected areas charge one per person, sometimes per visit.

Checked baggage on promo fares

A ₱999 seat sale can double once you add a 20 kg bag at the airport.

Tricycle and habal-habal rides

Short rides add up fast for a group of four. Agree on the price before you ride.

Island-hopping add-ons

Snorkel rental, lunch, and boat entrance fees are often on top of the tour price.

ATM fees and cash shortages

Some islands have few ATMs and frequent outages. Bring enough cash for 2 days.

Where to book

We'll tell you when booking direct is cheaper

Booking sites pay us a small commission. Small inns often give a better rate if you message them, and we say so.

Booking site

Best for free cancellation, paying by card, and reading many reviews in one place.

Direct (Facebook page or call)

Often 5–15% cheaper at small inns. Ask for a written confirmation and pay only a deposit.

Walk-in

Only off-peak, and only with a backup booked. Never during Holy Week or Christmas.

Trust rules

  • Every guide shows a full receipt, fees included
  • Say when booking direct is cheaper, even with no commission
  • No free stays in exchange for reviews; pay for our own trips
  • Date every price and refresh before summer, Holy Week, and Christmas
  • Disclosure next to booking links, not only in the footer
  • Clear email consent and easy unsubscribe (Data Privacy Act)

Never promote

  • Casino resorts or betting packages
  • "Travel club" MLMs and pay-to-join booking memberships
  • Timeshare presentations or "free vacation" sales pitches
  • Travel loans or "buy now, pay later" pushed as a way to afford a trip
  • Tour operators without a DOT accreditation we can verify
