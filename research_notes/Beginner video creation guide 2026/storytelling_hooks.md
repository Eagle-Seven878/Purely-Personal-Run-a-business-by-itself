# Storytelling, Hooks & CTAs: What Makes Short-Form / Social Video Resonate (2026)

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[RESEARCH]** = peer-reviewed / academic study or meta-analysis
- **[PLATFORM DATA]** = first-party platform or vendor research (useful but self-interested, usually not peer-reviewed)
- **[INDUSTRY]** = agency/vendor/aggregator stat, often unverifiable at the original source
- **[FOLK WISDOM]** = creator framework or practitioner heuristic; not experimentally tested

Research note: several primary pages (wistia.com, searchengineland.com, journals.sagepub.com, dl.acm.org) were blocked from direct fetch in this session, so some figures come via search-result summaries. These are flagged where relevant.

## Q1. Evidence on storytelling, emotion, curiosity, and persuasion

### Takeaway
The strongest research says: (1) stories persuade through "transportation" (absorption + empathy + imagery), which lowers counter-arguing; (2) high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety, amusement) drive sharing more than low-arousal ones (sadness); (3) curiosity is triggered by a *small, specific* knowledge gap, not by total mystery. Paul Zak's oxytocin-and-story claims are popular but rest on a biological mechanism (oxytocin → trust) that has **failed to replicate well**. Use his *behavioural* point (a dramatic arc beats a flat narrative) and drop the neurochemistry.

### Cited Findings

**Narrative transportation (strong, meta-analytic evidence) [RESEARCH]**
- Van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014), *Journal of Consumer Research* 40:797–817, "The Extended Transportation-Imagery Model": a meta-analysis of 9 unpublished and 327 published articles. Defines transportation as the extent to which a consumer (1) empathizes with the story's characters and (2) has their imagination activated by the plot, so that they experience "suspended reality" while taking in the story — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255699116_The_Extended_Transportation-Imagery_Model_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Antecedents_and_Consequences_of_Consumers'_Narrative_Transportation)
- Studies in that meta-analysis measured transportation with the Green & Brock (2000) Transportation scale, among others — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255699116_The_Extended_Transportation-Imagery_Model_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Antecedents_and_Consequences_of_Consumers'_Narrative_Transportation)
- A later meta-analysis looks at moderators of the transportation effect in digital storytelling: "Storytelling in the digital era: A meta-analysis of relevant moderators of the narrative transportation effect" (*Journal of Business Research*) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296318305356)
- Green & Appel (2024) have a review of how stories shape beliefs (preprint) — [Uni Würzburg PDF](https://www.mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110300/2024/Pdfs/Green___Appel__2024__Advances_Preprint.pdf); overview at [Wikipedia: Transportation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_theory_(psychology))

**Emotion and sharing: Berger & Milkman (2012) [RESEARCH]**
- "What Makes Online Content Viral?" appeared in the *Journal of Marketing Research*. The data set was every *New York Times* article over about three months, roughly 7,000 articles, with "most emailed" status as the outcome — [SAGE/JMR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353); [author PDF](http://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ViralityB.pdf)
- Physiological arousal partly drives virality. Content that evokes high-arousal positive emotion (awe) or high-arousal negative emotion (anger, anxiety) is more viral. Low-arousal, deactivating emotions such as sadness make content less viral — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1528077)
- Positive content was more viral than negative content overall. The results held after controlling for how surprising, interesting, or practically useful the content was (all three are also positively linked to sharing) and for how prominently it was featured — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1528077)

**TikTok-specific emotion evidence (partly conflicts with "positive wins") [RESEARCH]**
- Cheng & Li (2024), *Social Science Computer Review* 42(1):201–223, studied 101,292 TikTok news videos. They used multimodal sentiment analysis on the spoken words plus facial analysis to detect "second-person view", where the speaker talks straight to camera and addresses the audience. Negative sentiment and more second-person shots were associated with significantly higher engagement — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/08944393231178603); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371037272_Like_Comment_and_Share_on_TikTok_Exploring_the_Effect_of_Sentiment_and_Second-Person_View_on_the_User_Engagement_with_TikTok_News_Videos)
  - Both effects shrank or disappeared for news *publisher* accounts and were stronger for individual creators. Second-person view was linked to *fewer* comments on publisher videos but *more* comments on creator videos — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371037272_Like_Comment_and_Share_on_TikTok_Exploring_the_Effect_of_Sentiment_and_Second-Person_View_on_the_User_Engagement_with_TikTok_News_Videos)
  - CAUTION: a search summary attributed "+23.7% engagement for negative sentiment" and "+18.4% shares for second-person framing" to this paper. I could not open the full text to confirm these figures, so treat them as unverified.
- Another multimodal TikTok study found that visual feature complexity and color variety have **inverted-U** relationships with engagement: a moderate amount is best and too much hurts. Viewers' emotional responses mediated these effects — [Engagement Patterns in TikTok short video ads, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3648188.3677048) (via search summary; full text blocked)

**Berger's STEPPS (book framework built partly on research) [RESEARCH-INFORMED / FRAMEWORK]**
- *Contagious* (2013) names six drivers of sharing: Social Currency (sharing makes me look good), Triggers (top of mind, tip of tongue), Emotion ("when we care, we share", with arousal as the key), Public (visible behaviour gets imitated), Practical Value (news you can use), and Stories (a "Trojan horse" that carries the message) — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/contagious-jonah-berger-on-why-things-catch-on/); [consumerresearcher.com](https://consumerresearcher.com/jonah-berger-contagious)

**Curiosity / information-gap theory [RESEARCH]**
- Loewenstein (1994), "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation", *Psychological Bulletin* 116:75–98. Curiosity arises when attention is drawn to a gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. It is a reference-point phenomenon — [SCIRP reference](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=2828034); [Psychology Fanatic summary](https://psychologyfanatic.com/information-gap-theory/)
- Curiosity peaks when a person already has *some* knowledge, and it falls off when they know too little or too much — [arXiv clickbait-curiosity model citing Loewenstein](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.04212)
- Caveat: a 2023 study in a work setting found that information gaps increase curiosity but also feed frustration. Gaps left open too long or too wide backfire — [ScienceDirect, OBHDP 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749597823000523)

**Paul Zak: oxytocin, the dramatic arc, and donations [CONTESTED]**
- Zak's lab showed viewers a short film about a father whose two-year-old son, Ben, is dying of brain cancer. He reports that dramatic-arc stories (Freytag's structure) produce cortisol during rising tension and oxytocin during empathic resolution, and that oxytocin levels predicted donations — [Greater Good Science Center](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_stories_change_brain); [Future of StoryTelling](https://futureofstorytelling.org/case-study/paul-zak-empathy-neurochemistry-and-the-dramatic-arc/)
- A widely repeated claim says UK participants who watched PSAs with a strong narrative arc were "261% more likely to give". It appears in secondary summaries, and I did not trace it to a peer-reviewed primary source — [Storytelling Edge](https://storytellingedge.substack.com/p/the-surprising-science-of-storytelling)
- **Contradicting evidence:** Nave, Camerer & McCullough (2015), *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, conclude that the "simplest promising finding" (intranasal oxytocin increases trust) "has not replicated well". They also report that peripheral (blood) oxytocin measurement is flawed and that the cumulative evidence does not reliably tie trust to oxytocin — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691615600138)
- A preregistered replication in 2020 (*Nature Human Behaviour*), with more than 95% power, found no effect of oxytocin on trusting behaviour — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-0878-x); see also [PLOS One failed replication](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137000)

**Parasocial relationships [RESEARCH]**
- A meta-analysis, "How Parasocial Interactions Drive Online Purchase Intentions", reports that parasocial interaction builds trust in influencers and raises purchase intentions — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394154526_HOW_PARASOCIAL_INTERACTIONS_DRIVE_ONLINE_PURCHASE_INTENTIONS_A_META-ANALYSIS)
- Attitude homophily ("they think like me") and *social* attractiveness had the largest effects on parasocial relationships. Physical attractiveness had the least — [Emerging Science Journal](https://ijournalse.org/index.php/ESJ/article/view/3131)
- One study found that influencer credibility plus parasocial relationships explained 63.3% of the variance in trust. Parasocial effects are stronger for low-involvement, habitual purchases — [Emerging Science Journal](https://ijournalse.org/index.php/ESJ/article/view/3131)
- A 2026 *Frontiers in Communication* study found that parasocial relationships and trust mediate influencer-driven purchase intention — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1911649/full)

### Inferences
- For beginners, the most defensible "science of resonance" is: **a character the viewer relates to + causal tension + a resolution, delivered with a high-arousal feeling (awe, surprise, amusement, righteous anger, or anxiety followed by relief)**. Leaning on sadness alone is less likely to get shares.
- The TikTok evidence (negative sentiment and direct address) and Berger & Milkman's "positive > negative" can both hold. Arousal is the common factor, and platform and format moderate valence. A practical reading: open on a problem, frustration, or threat (arousal), then resolve toward something useful or hopeful.
- Talking directly to camera ("you") is one of the few hook and delivery choices with correlational evidence at scale behind it (Cheng & Li). For solo creators it builds the parasocial bond that the purchase-intention research links to trust.
- Guides should present Zak as "a dramatic arc beats a flat story" and not as "stories release oxytocin, which makes people trust you".

### Gaps
- There is no peer-reviewed, experimental study comparing named hook *types* (question vs. bold claim vs. visual) on short-form retention. The evidence is correlational or vendor-run.
- I could not verify the exact effect sizes in Cheng & Li (paywalled) or the primary source of Zak's "261%" figure.

## Q2. Proven hook types and formulas (creator frameworks)

### Takeaway
Platform data backs the claim that the first 1–3 seconds decide most outcomes. The specific hook formulas (Kallaway, Hormozi, Kane, MrBeast) are practitioner frameworks drawn from each creator's own analytics, not controlled studies. They converge on three things: **say what the video is about right away, add contrast or tension, and open a curiosity gap you promise to close.**

### Cited Findings

**Why the first seconds matter [PLATFORM DATA / INDUSTRY]**
- TikTok for Business research: 63% of the highest-performing videos "earn their audience" within the first three seconds. The figure is widely repeated in secondary sources — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/); [Lemonlight](https://www.lemonlight.com/blog/the-top-10-hooks-businesses-can-use-to-make-tiktok-marketing-work/)
- A MediaScience study for TikTok found that TikTok ads draw attention and physiological engagement in the first few seconds. Ads on screen for 6 seconds or less still delivered 38% of the recall of ads viewed for 20+ seconds — [MediaScience](https://www.mediascience.com/case-studies/understanding-attention-on-tiktok-ads/); [NMSBA](https://nmsba.com/news/1000-understanding-attention-on-tiktok-ads-drive-strong-brand-impact-across-view-durations)
- An academic analysis of TikTok ads found that most viewers leave within the first quarter of the video — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3648188.3677048) (via search summary)
- Claims such as "the algorithm makes its initial call at about 1.5 seconds" and "90% of underperforming videos fail in the first 3 seconds" appear on agency blogs with no primary source. Treat them as unverified [INDUSTRY] — [Clouted](https://clouted.com/blog/tiktok-hook-performance-statistics); [Hansen](https://hansencommerce.com/insights-tiktok-hook-3-seconds)

**MrBeast production memo (leaked internal team document, 2024) [FOLK WISDOM, from a top operator]**
- His three core metrics are CTR (click-through rate), AVD (average view duration) and AVP (average view percentage) — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- The first minute is the most important. Its job is to prove the thumbnail or title promise will be delivered. The memo treats losing 21M of 60M clickers in the first minute as a "reasonably good" result — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/mrbeast-leaked-pdf-spills-secrets-how-to-be-successful-on-youtube/)
- Minutes 1–3 move from hype into "crazy progression" to get viewers "super invested". Around minute 3 there is a "re-engagement" moment — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- "CTR is what dictates what we do for videos." His example contrasts "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" (lame) with "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" (clickable) — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/mrbeast-leaked-pdf-spills-secrets-how-to-be-successful-on-youtube/)

**Kallaway (Callaway), short-form hook formula [FOLK WISDOM]**
- Three steps: (1) **Context lean-in**, which says clearly what the video is about; (2) **Scroll-stop interjection**, a contrasting word or phrase that jolts the viewer ("but", "except", "here's the problem"); (3) **Contrarian snapback**, an unexpected twist that pulls attention back and creates curiosity about the payoff — [Recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-irresistible-hooks-and-blow-up-your-content-by-kallaway); [Podwise](https://app.podwise.ai/dashboard/episodes/2639392)
- He also stresses visual hooks and short, staccato sentences — [OpenTools summary](https://opentools.ai/youtube-summary/how-to-create-irresistible-hooks-and-blow-up-your-content)

**Alex Hormozi: Hook, Retain, Reward (*$100M Leads*, 2023) [FOLK WISDOM]**
- **Hook**: headline or visual. Topic sources are the far past, recent past, present, trending, or manufactured. Headline components are Recency, Relevancy, Celebrity, Proximity, Conflict, Unusual, Ongoing — [Business Book Club summary](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex); [itsmostly](https://itsmostly.com/blog/alex-hormozis-content-strategy-hook-retain-and-reward-explained)
- **Retain**: lists, steps, or stories that act as a "greased chute" pulling the viewer through — [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)
- **Reward**: fully satisfy the reason they clicked. Deliver high value per second — [itsmostly](https://itsmostly.com/blog/alex-hormozis-content-strategy-hook-retain-and-reward-explained)
- "Give, give, give, ask": build goodwill before any ask — [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)

**Brendan Kane, *Hook Point: How to Stand Out in a 3-Second World* [FOLK WISDOM]**
- A hook point is a short, compelling message or visual (text, insight, concept, personality, performance, or product) that grabs attention in about 3 seconds. His classic example is Netflix's "No Late Fees" against Blockbuster — [Baylor Keller Center](https://kellercenter.hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/insider-hook-point-how-stand-out-3-second-world); [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@lindokuhle.hlongwa/book-summary-for-hook-point-how-to-stand-out-in-a-3-second-world-by-brendan-kane-59964caaa6b4)
- He recommends testing many hook variants with data and keeping the winners — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@lindokuhle.hlongwa/book-summary-for-hook-point-how-to-stand-out-in-a-3-second-world-by-brendan-kane-59964caaa6b4)

**Faces and expressions in thumbnails / opening frames [INDUSTRY, weak evidence]**
- Claims such as "faces give 30–40% higher CTR", "surprise expression +43%" and "right-side face +11%" come from a thumbnail-tool vendor with no disclosed method. Treat them as unverified — [thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/face-in-youtube-thumbnail/)
- Peer-reviewed support is thin but directional. A conference study on food-video thumbnails found that the placement of key elements (faces, text) affects engagement — [ACM ICDMIT](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3808707.3808751)

### Inferences
Hook types that recur across these frameworks, each tied to the mechanism behind it:
1. **Curiosity gap / open loop** ("The reason your X isn't working has nothing to do with Y"): Loewenstein information gap.
2. **Contrarian / myth-bust** ("Stop doing X"): contrast plus arousal (Kallaway snapback; Hormozi's Conflict).
3. **Result-first / proof** ("I went from 0 to 10k in 30 days. Here's the exact order I did it"): social currency and practical value (STEPPS).
4. **Callout / identity** ("If you're a [niche] who [pain], watch this"): relevance and homophily (parasocial research).
5. **Stakes / challenge** ("I gave myself 24 hours to…"): MrBeast-style progression.
6. **Visual pattern interrupt**: an unusual first frame, movement, or prop. This is the "unusual" element, but a moderate amount of visual complexity is best (inverted U).
7. **Direct-address question** ("Do you ever…?"): second-person view (Cheng & Li).

### Gaps
- No independent test compares these named frameworks head to head. The "63% within 3 seconds" figure is TikTok's own and its method isn't public.
- Kallaway's full hook database and workshop are paywalled. The details above come from summaries of his free talks.

## Q3. Short-form story structures

### Takeaway
All the common short-form structures are compressed versions of one causal arc: **tension → escalation → resolution**. That is what the transportation research and Zak's arc findings reward. The practical rule with the best reputation is Parker & Stone's "but/therefore" test for causality. Open loops apply the curiosity-gap theory inside the video.

### Cited Findings
- **"But/Therefore" (South Park rule) [FOLK WISDOM from professional writers]**: At NYU Tisch in 2011 (mtvU's *Stand In*), Trey Parker and Matt Stone said the words between story beats should be "therefore" or "but". If "and then" fits, the story fails. They outline episode beats specifically to check "causation between each beat" — [Speakola transcript](https://speakola.com/arts/matt-stone-trey-parker-nyu-writing-class-2014); [Storytelling Edge](https://storytellingedge.substack.com/p/the-golden-rule-of-storytelling-according); [YouTube clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9jEg9uiLOU)
- **Dramatic arc (Freytag)**: Zak's work contrasted arc-structured stories with flat narratives, and the arc versions produced more attention and empathy responses. The mechanism is contested (see Q1) — [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_stories_change_brain)
- **Transportation drivers**: identifiable characters (empathy) and vivid imagery (imagination) are the antecedents of persuasion through story — [Van Laer et al. 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255699116_The_Extended_Transportation-Imagery_Model_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Antecedents_and_Consequences_of_Consumers'_Narrative_Transportation)
- **Re-engagement / progression**: the MrBeast memo puts a re-hook around minute 3 and keeps escalation ("crazy progression") going so viewers stay invested — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- **Retain via lists, steps, stories**: Hormozi's "greased chute" — [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)
- **Unpredictability**: research on TikTok's recommender argues that surprise and unpredictability are essential to sustained interest alongside prediction — [Salles 2025, New Media & Society](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448251385086) (via search summary)

### Inferences (structures, all [FOLK WISDOM] copywriting frameworks mapped onto the research above)
- **PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solve)**: name the pain, then make it vivid and emotionally arousing (anxiety or frustration is high arousal), then resolve. This matches arousal → relief.
- **Hook–Story–Lesson–CTA**: hook (gap) → short causal story (transportation) → one takeaway (practical value) → one ask.
- **Before–After–Bridge (BAB)**: show the "before" state, then the "after" state (result-first creates a gap: *how?*), then the bridge (the method). Opening on the "after" works well as a hook.
- **But/Therefore chain**: write 3–6 beats and join them only with "but" or "therefore". Every "but" is a mini pattern interrupt.
- **Open loops**: tease a payoff early ("the third one surprised me") and close it later. Keep the gap small and specific, and don't leave it open too long (the frustration caveat in Q1).
- **Pattern interrupts**: change the shot, angle, B-roll, on-screen text, or sound every few seconds. Keep it moderate, because the TikTok study found an inverted U for visual complexity.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled research directly comparing PAS, BAB, and Hook–Story–Lesson in video. These are copywriting conventions.

## Q4. CTA best practices

### Takeaway
Evidence for "one CTA" is weaker than creators claim. The famous jam-study "choice overload" effect is conditional and averaged close to zero in meta-analysis, though reducing friction and ambiguity is still sound practice. Comment-keyword to auto-DM CTAs report strong numbers, but only from vendors. Soft CTAs (follow, comment, save) suit value content. Hard CTAs (buy, book) work better after trust is built, which fits Hormozi's "give before ask" and the parasocial-trust research.

### Cited Findings
- **Choice overload**: Iyengar & Lepper's 2000 jam study found that 6 jams led to a 30% purchase rate against 3% for 24 jams — [Atticus Li](https://atticusli.com/replication-crisis/choice-overload-jam-study/)
- **Contradicting**: Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd's (2010) meta-analysis of 50 experiments found a mean effect near zero. Choice overload depends on domain, preference clarity, time pressure, and how options are displayed — [ResearchGate re-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256846155_Re-Analyzing_a_Meta-Analysis_Another_Look_at_Choice_Overload); Chernev et al.'s (2015) meta-analysis identifies the moderators — [Chernev PDF](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)
- **Comment-keyword → DM [INDUSTRY/VENDOR]**: the mechanic is "Comment GUIDE", after which an auto-DM delivers the link or lead magnet at peak intent — [ManyChat](https://get.manychat.com/use-case/comment-to-dm); [ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/how-course-creators-use-instagram-dm-automation/)
  - The claims "comment-to-DM gets 3–5× higher conversion than link-in-bio (ManyChat 2024 platform report, 1M+ campaigns)" and "niche-specific triggers get 3.1× revenue" come from third-party blogs citing ManyChat. I did not verify them against the primary report — [dmtracker.ai](https://dmtracker.ai/manychat-instagram-dm-automation)
  - The claim of "70–90% DM open rates, up to 60% reply rates" comes from vendor or affiliate content — [unkoa](https://www.unkoa.com/instagram-dm-automation-vs-email-in-2025-why-manychat-delivers-90-open-rates-and-60-reply-rates/); [communipass](https://communipass.com/blog/auto-dm-statistics-2026-open-rates-conversion-benchmarks/)
- **Give before ask**: Hormozi's rule is to build a "bank account" of goodwill with value before any ask — [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)
- **Trust mediates purchase**: parasocial relationships and trust mediate influencer-to-purchase effects — [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1911649/full)
- **Success metrics are shifting**: Wistia's 2026 State of Video (nearly 1,000 marketers, 13M+ videos) found that "social engagement" as a success metric rose from 12% to 22% in one year — [Wistia blog (via search)](https://wistia.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics); [HubSpot recap](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/state-of-video-marketing-new-data)

### Inferences
- Beginner rule: **one primary action per video**, chosen by the video's job. For reach videos, ask for a follow, share, or save ("send this to the friend who…" draws on STEPPS social currency and practical value). For lead videos, use a comment keyword. For sales videos, use a direct link or book-a-call, but only after value content.
- Put the CTA where the payoff lands (right after the reward). An abrupt ask before the promise is delivered breaks the Hook–Retain–Reward contract.
- Comment-keyword CTAs also feed engagement signals (comments). That is a plausible reason they help distribution, but platforms haven't confirmed it.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed data on CTA placement or type in short-form video. The Wistia CTA and annotation conversion benchmarks could not be fetched (domain blocked).

## Q5. Authenticity: polished vs lo-fi/UGC, face vs faceless, speaking to one person

### Takeaway
Industry data consistently favors native, phone-shot, creator-style video on TikTok, Reels and Shorts for hook rate and trust. The evidence is mostly from platforms and agencies. Direct-to-camera address ("you") has large-sample correlational support on TikTok, especially for individual creators. The parasocial research explains why a consistent human face builds trust. "Faceless" content has no comparable evidence base in its favor.

### Cited Findings
- Self-recorded (lo-fi) story-ad creative beat studio-shot creative on hook rate 84% of the time. 42% of top-spending social ads use lo-fi approaches. 81% of e-commerce marketers say real-customer visuals outperform polished content [INDUSTRY; originally cited by Search Engine Land and agencies; primary studies not verified] — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ugly-ads-outperforming-polished-creative-475007); [Creative Clicks](https://www.creativeclicks.com/blog/ugly-ads-beautiful-roas-the-lo-fi-creative-effect-and-when-to-ditch-polish)
- Creator-style videos mirror native TikTok use (handheld framing, quick hooks, casual talk) and earn attention more efficiently than studio assets [PLATFORM DATA/INDUSTRY] — [Carnegie](https://www.carnegiehighered.com/tiktok-ads-for-colleges-video-creation/); [TikAdTools](https://tikadtools.com/blog/tiktok-ads-creative/)
- Caveat: the Creative Clicks piece is explicitly framed as "when to ditch polish", meaning polish can still win in some contexts (luxury, brand) — [Creative Clicks](https://www.creativeclicks.com/blog/ugly-ads-beautiful-roas-the-lo-fi-creative-effect-and-when-to-ditch-polish)
- Wistia 2026: the bar for what "good" looks like keeps rising even as more non-video professionals get on camera. LinkedIn is now the #1 B2B video channel (81%). Podcast video grew 29% [PLATFORM/VENDOR SURVEY] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-beats-youtube-as-top-b2b-video-channel-wistia-report-finds/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/state-of-video-marketing-new-data)
- **Speaking to one person / direct address [RESEARCH]**: more second-person-view shots are associated with higher engagement across 101,292 TikTok videos, more strongly for individual creators than for institutional publishers — [Cheng & Li 2024](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371037272_Like_Comment_and_Share_on_TikTok_Exploring_the_Effect_of_Sentiment_and_Second-Person_View_on_the_User_Engagement_with_TikTok_News_Videos)
- **Face / human connection [RESEARCH]**: parasocial relationships (driven by attitude similarity and social attractiveness, not looks) raise trust and purchase intention — [Emerging Science Journal](https://ijournalse.org/index.php/ESJ/article/view/3131); [meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394154526_HOW_PARASOCIAL_INTERACTIONS_DRIVE_ONLINE_PURCHASE_INTENTIONS_A_META-ANALYSIS)

### Inferences
- For a beginner, "raw but clear" is the evidence-aligned default: phone camera, good light, clean audio, captions, talking to one person. Invest in audio and clarity before cinematic polish.
- Showing your face and using "you" language is supported by both the parasocial and the direct-address evidence. Faceless formats can work for reach, but they give up the trust mechanism.
- Individual creators get *more* benefit from direct address and emotion than institutional accounts do, which favors a personal-brand style over a corporate voice.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous head-to-head study of faceless vs. face-on-camera channels. The lo-fi statistics are mostly agency-sourced, and their primary studies (sample, method) could not be verified.

## Q6. Ready-to-use templates for beginners

### Takeaway
These templates are **[FOLK WISDOM] syntheses**. Each line is tied to the mechanism from Q1–Q5 that justifies it, so beginners know *why* it works. Test several hooks per idea, as Kane and MrBeast advise.

### Cited Findings (basis for the templates)
- Curiosity gap (Loewenstein) — [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=2828034); contrast plus snapback (Kallaway) — [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-irresistible-hooks-and-blow-up-your-content-by-kallaway); Hook/Retain/Reward (Hormozi) — [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex); but/therefore — [Speakola](https://speakola.com/arts/matt-stone-trey-parker-nyu-writing-class-2014); direct address — [Cheng & Li](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371037272_Like_Comment_and_Share_on_TikTok_Exploring_the_Effect_of_Sentiment_and_Second-Person_View_on_the_User_Engagement_with_TikTok_News_Videos); first-minute promise check (MrBeast) — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences: Fill-in-the-blank hook lines (grouped by mechanism)
**Curiosity gap**
- "The reason your ___ isn't working has nothing to do with ___."
- "Nobody tells you this about ___ until it's too late."
- "I tried ___ for ___ days. Day ___ changed everything."
**Contrarian / Kallaway pattern (context → "but" → twist)**
- "Everyone says ___ to get ___. **But** that's exactly why you're stuck. Do ___ instead."
- "Stop ___. Seriously. Here's what to do instead."
**Result-first / Before-After**
- "I went from ___ to ___ in ___. Here's the exact ___ I used."
- "This is what ___ looked like before. This is after. Here's the one thing I changed."
**Callout / identity (direct address)**
- "If you're a ___ who ___, this is for you."
- "You're probably making this ___ mistake right now."
**Stakes / challenge**
- "I have ___ [time/money] to ___. Let's see if it's possible."
**List / practical value**
- "3 ___ I wish I knew before ___. Number 3 is the one nobody talks about." (open loop)

Hook checklist: (1) the topic is clear within 1–2 seconds; (2) there is tension or contrast; (3) the promise is specific; (4) text on screen matches the spoken words; (5) the first frame is visually interesting but not cluttered.

### Inferences: Script skeletons
**30-second (1 idea, reach or follow)**
- 0–3s HOOK: curiosity gap or callout, said to camera with on-screen text.
- 3–8s CONTEXT: why this matters to *you* (the stakes).
- 8–24s VALUE / STORY: 2–3 beats joined by "but" or "therefore", or a 3-step list. Change the visual every 2–4 seconds.
- 24–28s PAYOFF: close the loop from the hook.
- 28–30s ONE SOFT CTA: "Follow for part 2", "Save this", or "Send it to a friend who ___."

**60-second (Hook–Story–Lesson–CTA or PAS)**
- 0–3s HOOK (result-first or contrarian).
- 3–10s PROBLEM: name the pain in the viewer's words.
- 10–20s AGITATE / STAKES: what happens if nothing changes, **but**…
- 20–45s STORY / SOLUTION: your moment of struggle (**but**), the discovery (**therefore**), 2–3 specific steps. Add a mini re-hook at around 25s ("and the last step is the one most people skip").
- 45–53s LESSON / PAYOFF: one sentence the viewer can repeat.
- 53–60s ONE CTA: a comment keyword ("Comment PLAN and I'll send you the template") or a follow.

**3-minute (YouTube / LinkedIn / long Reels, MrBeast-style structure scaled down)**
- 0–10s HOOK + PROMISE: say exactly what they'll get and show a glimpse of the result.
- 10–30s PROOF THE PROMISE IS REAL: credibility, a before/after, or stakes. This matches the memo's point that the first minute must confirm the thumbnail's promise.
- 30s–1:30 PART 1: the first beat or step, ending with an open loop.
- ~1:30 RE-ENGAGEMENT: a surprise, a twist ("**but** then ___ happened"), or the most impressive moment.
- 1:30–2:30 PART 2–3: escalate (progression), each segment causally linked.
- 2:30–2:50 PAYOFF + LESSON: close every loop and give one clear takeaway.
- 2:50–3:00 ONE CTA, matched to the video's job (soft for value videos, hard only after trust is built).

**Before-After-Bridge micro template (15–30s)**
- "This was me ___ [before]. Now ___ [after]. The bridge was ___ [3 steps]. [CTA]."

### Gaps
- None of these templates has been A/B tested in published research. Users should A/B test hooks themselves: several first lines per idea, compared on 3-second hold rate and average view duration.
