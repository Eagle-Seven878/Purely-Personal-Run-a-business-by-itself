## 1. Filming basics with a phone (lighting, audio, framing, settings)

### Takeaway
Order of priority for a beginner: (1) clean audio (mic close to mouth, soft room), (2) soft front/45-degree light from a window, never behind you, (3) back camera at eye level with gridlines, locked exposure/focus, (4) 1080p/30fps is enough for vertical social video; 4K helps only if you plan to crop or shoot horizontal.

### Cited Findings
**Audio (the top priority)**
- Viewers are "much less forgiving of bad sound than bad visuals"; use an external mic if possible and avoid wind or use a windshield — [Grafdom mobile videography tips](https://www.grafdom.com/mobile-videography-tips-beginners/) / [Ivy Group](https://ivygroup.com/blog/best-smartphone-video/) (search summary across beginner mobile guides)
- "Bad sound kills a video faster than a blurry image"; built-in phone/laptop mics pick up cars, air conditioners and wind — fix with an external mic — [Ulanzi: beginner vlogging mistakes](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/news/beginner-vlogging-mistakes) / [VideoAuteur](https://www.videoauteur.com/what-are-the-most-common-beginner-video-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them.html)
- Mic distance: roughly 6–8 inches from the mouth; in small echoey rooms get closer (about 15–25 cm). Lavalier (clip-on) mics stay a few inches from the mouth, cutting room pickup versus a desk or camera mic — [DJI: wireless lavalier audio quality](https://www.dji.com/media-center/insights/how-to-get-best-audio-quality-from-wireless-lavalier-microphone); [Hollyland: less echoey mic](https://www.hollyland.com/blog/topics/make-your-mic-sound-less-echoey); [Audio-Technica lavalier basics](https://www.audio-technica.com/en-us/support/basic-audio-techniques-video-selecting-right-lavalier-mic)
- If the mic is on the camera and the camera is more than 5–10 feet away, you'll capture lots of ambient noise — [Wistia: reduce echo in your video](https://wistia.com/learn/production/soundproofing-for-video) (via search summary)
- Room echo fixes: record in rooms with carpet, curtains, soft furniture; hang 3–4 moving blankets (typically under $20 each), one on the floor and one on each side of the speaker; "distance plus softness is the fastest combo" — [Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/production/soundproofing-for-video); [Hollyland](https://www.hollyland.com/blog/topics/make-your-mic-sound-less-echoey)

**Lighting**
- Key light at 30–45 degrees to the side, slightly above eye level, angled down; 45 degrees is standard — [Vimeo: home video lighting tips](https://vimeo.com/blog/post/video-lighting-tips); [OBSBOT lighting for vlogging 2026](https://www.obsbot.com/blog/vlog/lighting-for-vlogging)
- Window as key: turn about 45 degrees to the window for a flattering shadow on one side; use a daylight lamp or white foam board on the other side as fill; a lamp behind can act as backlight — [Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/blog/post/video-lighting-tips); [COLBOR home lighting](https://www.colborlight.com/blogs/articles/set-up-lighting-for-home-video-recording)
- Overcast/cloudy light is softer and more flattering than direct sun; keep lighting consistent through a shoot; ring lights are useful indoors — [Ivy Group](https://ivygroup.com/blog/best-smartphone-video/); [Grafdom](https://www.grafdom.com/mobile-videography-tips-beginners/)
- Strong backlight (window behind you), uneven light and overhead shadows are key beginner mistakes; use soft front-facing light at eye level (window, softbox, ring light, or a desk lamp with a diffuser) — [Teleprompter.com: mistakes when speaking on video](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-speaking-on-video); [Videomaker: 4 lighting mistakes](https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/lighting/lighting-design/4-common-lighting-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them/)

**Framing and eye contact**
- Turn on gridlines and place the subject on the lines or intersections (rule of thirds) — [Grafdom](https://www.grafdom.com/mobile-videography-tips-beginners/)
- Look at the lens, not your own image on screen; a sticky note or small object near the lens helps — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-speaking-on-video)

**Phone settings**
- Lock focus and exposure: tap and hold the screen until "AE/AF Lock" appears, then drag up/down to adjust brightness; this stops the camera from "hunting" mid-shot — [Grafdom](https://www.grafdom.com/mobile-videography-tips-beginners/); [Apple: change video recording settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-video-recording-settings-iphc1827d32f/ios)
- Reels, TikTok and Shorts transcode and often downscale uploads, so much 4K detail is never seen; 1080p is fine for everyday social clips and saves storage; use 4K when you plan to crop or edit heavily (for example, cutting a vertical short out of a horizontal shot) — [VideoProc: best iPhone video settings](https://www.videoproc.com/iphone-video-processing/best-iphone-video-quality-settings.htm); [Switcher Studio: iPhone settings](https://www.switcherstudio.com/blog/best-iphone-camera-settings-for-video) (search summaries; some supporting text came from a low-authority Alibaba "product insights" page, not relied on)
- Creator rule of thumb: "1080p at 30fps is the sweet spot" for social; 4K is the better option when shooting horizontally — [TikTok creator tip](https://www.tiktok.com/@tiktokstalman/video/7573395864748690699) (single creator's opinion)
- Some guides recommend landscape as the default orientation — [Ivy Group](https://ivygroup.com/blog/best-smartphone-video/) (this conflicts with vertical-first short-form practice; pick orientation by target platform)

### Inferences
- Recommended beginner default: rear camera (higher quality than the selfie camera on nearly all phones), 1080p or 4K at 30fps, gridlines on, AE/AF lock, phone on a tripod at eye level, window at 45 degrees in front/side, clip-on mic. Vertical 9:16 for Shorts/Reels/TikTok; horizontal 16:9 for YouTube long-form (or shoot 4K horizontal and crop).
- Rear-camera trick: to see yourself while using the rear camera, use a phone app/monitor, a mirror, or a teleprompter app that can mirror; otherwise check framing with a test clip.
- Headroom: keep a small gap (roughly a hand's width in frame) above the head and eyes on the upper third line. Standard practice; no 2026-specific source found.

### Gaps
- No primary-source (Apple/Google) confirmation found that rear cameras outperform front cameras on 2026 flagship phones. This is widely assumed but was not directly sourced.
- No sourced specific headroom rule; the upper-third eye-line guideline above is standard practice, not a cited finding.

## 2. Camera confidence, teleprompter use, short takes

### Takeaway
Confidence comes from volume of reps. Structured "30-day talking to camera" challenges (5+ minutes a day) are the standard creator-educator method. Record in short chunks and cut together; keep eye contact with the lens; trim filler words.

### Cited Findings
- The 30-Day Talking To Camera Challenge (Thomas Norman / Portal Creative): record at least 5 minutes of talking a day on your phone, with a daily lesson and prompt — [Portal Creative 30-day challenge](https://www.portalcreative.com/the-30-day-talking-to-camera-challenge); [program outline](https://www.portalcreative.com/30-day-talking-to-camera-challenge-full-program)
- Week 1 focus in such plans: self-introduction, eye contact with the lens, posture, smiling while telling happy memories, describing hobbies clearly, cutting filler words ("um", "like"). Participants report a visible difference between day 1 and day 10, plus more confidence in video meetings — [From Sarah Jolie free challenge](https://fromsarahjolie.com/talking-to-the-camera-challenge/); [Bevan's Bench outline](https://www.bevansbench.com/blog/30-day-talk-camera-challenge-outline)
- Other structured options: [StoryLab 30-Day Camera Confidence Challenge](https://storylab.co/confidence-challenge/), [Kyle Draper 30-Day Video Challenge](https://landing.kyledraper.com/30dayvideochallenge), [The Carty Method on-camera challenge](https://www.thecartymethod.com/30day-on-camera-challenge)
- Eye contact: look into the lens, not at your own image; practise short recordings focused only on eye contact — [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-when-speaking-on-video)

### Inferences
- Teleprompter use: put the text near the lens (teleprompter apps overlay scrolling text at the top of the screen near the camera), set a slow scroll, write in conversational bullet points rather than full paragraphs so you don't sound read-aloud. This practice is described by Teleprompter.com, but the details weren't fetched.
- Short takes: record one sentence or idea per take. If you stumble, pause, then restart the sentence. Jump cuts in editing hide the restarts, so perfection isn't needed.

### Gaps
- No controlled data found on how long it takes beginners to gain camera confidence; evidence is anecdotal/testimonial.

## 3. Step-by-step beginner editing workflow (CapCut or similar)

### Takeaway
A standard beginner edit takes six passes: set up the project, cut the fluff (split/trim/remove silences), add captions, add B-roll/zooms/text, set music about 18 dB under the voice, then export at 1080p (or 4K) 30fps. Important 2026 caveat: CapCut auto-captions reportedly moved behind the Pro paywall.

### Cited Findings
- Beginner CapCut workflow: set up the project, cut the fluff fast, then polish with captions, audio, colour and export settings — [CapCut Guide: first cut to export](https://capcutguide.com/capcut-tutorial/); [CapCut official beginner tutorial](https://www.capcut.com/resource/capcut-tutorial-for-beginners)
- Desktop CapCut shortcuts Q/W (trim left/right of the playhead) speed up cutting and silence removal — [Primal Video: CapCut for PC & Mac tutorial 2026](https://primalvideo.com/guides/capcut-for-pc-mac-tutorial/) (search summary)
- CapCut AutoCut / auto-cut tools detect and remove silences and pair with caption templates — [CapCut: AutoCut for videos](https://www.capcut.com/resource/autocut-for-videos); [Miracamp: AutoCut guide 2026](https://www.miracamp.com/learn/capcut/the-ultimate-guide-to-autocut)
- Auto captions: Text > Auto Captions transcribes speech into synced captions; tap to fix misheard words; apply one style to all captions. **Auto captions moved to the Pro plan in 2026** — [Pexo: CapCut tutorial 2026](https://pexo.ai/blog/capcut-tutorial-9296) / [Ruah Creative House CapCut 2026](https://www.ruahcreativehouse.org/blog/capcut-tutorial/) (paywall claim from secondary sources; could not verify on capcut.com)
- Export: choose resolution and frame rate on export; the file saves to the device — [CapCut official tutorial](https://www.capcut.com/resource/capcut-tutorial-for-beginners)
- Music level: start background music around **-18 dB below the voice**, then adjust by ear; music with vocals, heavy drums or strong mids may need to go lower. Other creators mix it roughly 10 dB under speech — [Pure Audio Insight: background music volume](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/background-music-volume-how-loud-should-it-be)
- YouTube normalises toward about **-14 LUFS** integrated loudness. A mix at -20 LUFS is left quiet, so aim near -14 LUFS without clipping peaks — [Pure Audio Insight: YouTube audio levels](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/perfect-youtube-audio-levels-creators-technical-guide); [Kevin Muldoon: right audio levels for YouTube](https://www.kevinmuldoon.com/audio-levels-youtube/); [Youlean](https://youlean.co/how-to-edit-a-video-to-achieve-good-audio-loudness-on-youtube/)
- Common editing mistakes: overusing transitions and effects, inconsistent audio levels, long intros — [Storyblocks: 8 editing mistakes](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/blog/editing-mistakes-beginners-make-with-video); [DL Sounds: 15 editing mistakes](https://www.dl-sounds.com/video-editing-mistakes/)

### Inferences
Suggested step-by-step for a writer to present (synthesised from the sources above; exact menu names vary by version):
1. New project; import clips; set the aspect ratio (9:16 short-form, 16:9 long-form).
2. Rough cut: split (Ctrl/Cmd+B on desktop, "Split" on mobile) and delete false starts; use Q/W on desktop; run silence removal/AutoCut, then review manually.
3. Jump cuts: cut out pauses and "ums" between sentences. To hide jarring jumps, alternate a 110–120% punch-in zoom on every other clip (a common creator technique; no specific source fetched).
4. Captions: auto captions (Pro) or free alternatives (TikTok/Instagram in-app captions, YouTube auto-captions, Descript/other tools); proofread names and jargon.
5. B-roll: place it on an overlay track above the talking-head clip when you say something showable; change the visual every few seconds in short-form.
6. Text overlays: a hook headline in the first 1–2 seconds; keep it inside safe zones (away from the bottom and right edges where platform UI sits).
7. Music: platform-safe library track at about -18 dB relative to the voice; duck it under speech.
8. Export at 1080p (or 4K), 30fps, matching the frame rate you shot.

## 4. Faceless video workflows

### Takeaway
Faceless formats (voiceover + B-roll, screen recording, hands-only, text-on-screen, slideshows, AI voice/avatar) lower the barrier to entry. The main risks in 2026 are YouTube's "inauthentic content" monetisation rule and AI-disclosure requirements. The human-added perspective, script and editing decisions are what keep a faceless channel compliant.

### Cited Findings
- Faceless videos use voiceover, screen recordings, stock footage, animation and editing. A typical beginner loop: research/script (hour 1), gather assets (hour 2), assemble and voice over (hour 3) — [Murf: start a faceless channel](https://murf.ai/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel); [Virlo: faceless tips](https://virlo.ai/blog/how-to-make-faceless-youtube-videos) (vendor blogs)
- Screen recording "production floor is low — a screen recorder and a voiceover is everything"; works best with cursor highlights and short text cues — [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/how-to-make-faceless-youtube-videos); [1of10: faceless channel with AI 2026](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel-with-ai/)
- Royalty-free B-roll/stock: Pexels, Mixkit — [Murf](https://murf.ai/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel)
- AI voice: ElevenLabs is described as the quality standard, but AI still stumbles on proper nouns, niche terms and ambiguous emphasis, so listen to the whole output before using it — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel-with-ai/); [Speechmatics faceless business guide](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/how-to-launch-a-successful-faceless-youtube-channel-for-business)
- Pros: no on-camera talent, lighting or retakes; cheaper gear; lower skill barrier. Cons: editing is the biggest challenge; expect 20–40 videos and a few months before traction or monetisation — [Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/how-to-launch-a-successful-faceless-youtube-channel-for-business); [ClipSpeed guide](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-guide.html)
- **YouTube "inauthentic content" policy**: YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in the YPP policy effective **July 15, 2025**. Mass-produced or repetitive videos that look template-made with little variation aren't eligible for monetisation — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [AlternativeTo news, July 2025](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content); [Fliki: July 2025 update](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025). (Note: one search summary misdated this to 2026; the URLs indicate July 2025.)
- A pipeline of AI script → AI voice → stock footage → upload with "zero human editorial decisions" is the pattern the policy targets. Faceless channels can still monetise if the content is original and adds value — [InVideo](https://invideo.io/blog/youtube-kills-ai-faceless-channels/); [Bottle Rocket 2026](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-2026/)
- Claimed enforcement ladder (warning, then 90-day suspension, then permanent YPP removal) — [search summary of secondary guides](https://vexub.com/blog/ai-generated-video-monetization-policies) (**unverified; not confirmed against YouTube Help**)

### Inferences
Step-by-step per faceless format (a practical synthesis):
- **Voiceover + B-roll:** script, then record the VO with a close mic in a soft room, then lay down the VO track, then cover each sentence with matching B-roll (own footage, Pexels/Mixkit), then add captions and music. Pro: fast, scalable. Con: stock looks generic, and there's inauthentic-content risk if you rely only on stock.
- **Screen recording (tutorials, software, finance, gaming):** OBS/QuickTime/built-in phone recorder, then VO, then zoom-ins on the cursor area. Pro: cheapest, high trust. Con: niche-limited.
- **Hands-only (cooking, crafts, unboxing, writing):** overhead phone mount, window light, lav mic or VO in post. Pro: personal and authentic without a face. Con: needs a physical setup.
- **Text-on-screen / slideshow / carousel video:** Canva or CapCut templates plus a trending/library sound. Pro: minimal skill. Con: lowest differentiation and most "template-like".
- **AI voice/avatar:** fastest but carries the most policy risk. Disclose realistic synthetic voices/people (see section 8) and add original research or opinion.

## 5. Batching and content systems; repurposing

### Takeaway
Batch by stage (ideas, then scripts, then filming, then editing) and repurpose one long recording into many shorts. Vendor data suggests 8–12 clips per hour of long-form content and 3–4 hours to cut 5–6 Shorts from a 20-minute video by hand.

### Cited Findings
- Manually repurposing a 20-minute video into 5–6 Shorts can take 3–4 hours; with efficient/AI workflows that drops sharply — [OpusClip: Shorts at scale](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-at-scale) (vendor)
- Systematic repurposers report 8–12 short clips per hour of long-form content; a 90-minute recording can supply a week of clips — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-at-scale); [Quso](https://quso.ai/blog/how-to-turn-long-form-videos-into-youtube-shorts-at-scale) (vendor)
- Workflow: record long-form, pull the transcript, mark clip moments, edit in assembly-line fashion; publish the batch of Shorts/Reels/TikToks over 1–3 weeks — [PostQuickAI repurposing guide](https://www.postquick.ai/blog/how-to-repurpose-video-content-into-shorts-reels-tiktok-youtube); [Shortzly: content batching 2026](https://shortzly.com/blog/content-batching-short-form-video)
- Paid repurposing tools ($20–50/month) usually pay for themselves in time saved (vendor claim) — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-at-scale)
- Keep a repeatable visual style (same B-roll sources, icons, caption style) across uploads for speed and consistency — [Murf](https://murf.ai/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel)
- Music licences don't transfer across platforms. TikTok library music is licensed for TikTok only, so re-uploading that video as a YouTube Short will likely get a Content ID claim. Repurpose clean exports without music, then add each platform's own library audio — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/can-i-use-tiktok-music-on-youtube-and-other-platforms-why-you-cant-and-what-to-do-about-it); [Foxi: Shorts music guide 2026](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-shorts-copyright-music-library-guide/)

### Inferences
Suggested beginner weekly system (about 5–6 hours total, estimated from the sources above):
- **Content pillars:** 3 recurring topics (for example, how-to / mistake / story). **Idea bank:** a notes app or spreadsheet where you log every audience question, comment and competitor hit.
- Mon: 60 min scripting 3–5 short scripts (hook, 3 points, CTA) from the bank.
- Tue: 60–90 min batch filming, in one outfit and one lighting setup.
- Wed–Thu: 2–3 h batch editing from a saved CapCut template (caption style, intro text, end card).
- Fri: 30 min scheduling, then 30 min reviewing analytics.
- Or record one 20–30 min long video/podcast and cut it into 5–10 shorts.
- Export a master without music so each platform can get licensed audio.

### Gaps
- Repurposing yield and time figures come from AI-clipping vendors with a commercial interest; no independent benchmark was found.

## 6. Beginner learning curves, 30-day practice plans, iterating from analytics

### Takeaway
Plan for 20–40 videos before meaningful traction. Use a 30-day daily-reps plan for skills. Improve by reading retention on the first 30 seconds and CTR, and change one variable per video.

### Cited Findings
- 30-day plans: 5+ minutes of on-camera talking a day, a new prompt/lesson each day, a week-1 focus on eye contact, posture and filler words; day-1 vs day-10 improvement is commonly reported — [Portal Creative](https://www.portalcreative.com/the-30-day-talking-to-camera-challenge); [From Sarah Jolie](https://fromsarahjolie.com/talking-to-the-camera-challenge/); [StoryLab](https://storylab.co/confidence-challenge/)
- Expect 20–40 videos and a few months before the algorithm and monetisation pay out (faceless context) — [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-guide.html) / [Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/how-to-launch-a-successful-faceless-youtube-channel-for-business) (search summary)
- The first 30 seconds shape the retention curve more than any other stretch; holding 70%+ of viewers at 30 seconds is described as strong — [Influencer Notes: first 30 seconds](https://influencernotes.com/academy/youtube-audience-retention-first-30-seconds); [SocialRails retention guide 2026](https://socialrails.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-complete-guide) (secondary; the 70% benchmark isn't from YouTube)
- Lead with something visual or straight to the point, not logos or context; if retention keeps dropping early, script a tighter hook — [Uppbeat retention tips](https://uppbeat.io/blog/youtube-growth/youtube-analytics/youtube-audience-retention); [Teleprompter.com retention 2026](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention)
- High CTR with low retention means the packaging promises something the video doesn't deliver. Test one variable per video — [Outfy: YouTube analytics 2026](https://www.outfy.com/blog/youtube-analytics/); [Awesome Creator Academy](https://www.awesomecreatoracademy.com/blog/youtube-analytics-guide)
- YouTube Studio's official key-moments/retention tools: [YouTube Help: key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)

### Inferences
Sample 30-day plan for the writer (a synthesis):
- **Week 1 (reps):** a 5-min daily talk-to-camera (unpublished) plus learning phone settings, lighting and mic.
- **Week 2 (edit skills):** publish 3 short videos; practise split, jump cut, captions and music levels.
- **Week 3 (system):** pick 3 pillars, build a 30-idea bank, batch-script and film 5 shorts in one session.
- **Week 4 (iterate):** publish 5; in analytics compare 3-second/30-second hold and average view duration; rewrite hooks for the worst performers; try one faceless format.

## 7. Most common beginner mistakes and fixes

### Takeaway
The biggest mistakes are bad audio, backlighting, not looking at the lens, long intros, auto-exposure/focus hunting, over-editing, unlicensed music and inconsistent posting. Each has a cheap fix.

- Long intros and filler → get to the point fast — [Ulanzi](https://www.ulanzi.com/blogs/news/beginner-vlogging-mistakes); [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/youtube-growth/youtube-analytics/youtube-audience-retention)

## 8. Legal basics: music copyright and AI disclosure

### Takeaway
Use each platform's own licensed library (YouTube Audio Library, TikTok Commercial Music Library, Instagram's library), or Content-ID-cleared royalty-free services. "Royalty-free" doesn't guarantee "claim-free". Disclose realistic AI-generated or altered people, voices and events on YouTube, TikTok and Meta. AI help with scripts, captions, thumbnails and colour correction doesn't need a label.

### Cited Findings
**Music**
- YouTube Audio Library tracks (in YouTube Studio) are "copyright-safe" and won't be claimed through Content ID; some tracks require attribution in the description (Creative Commons) — [YouTube Help: Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en); [Foxi: Audio Library guide 2026](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/copyright-free-music-youtube-audio-library-guide/)
- TikTok's Commercial Music Library is licensed for use on TikTok; only use songs available in-app — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/find-royalty-free-commercial-music-for-tiktok); [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/how-to-use-music-legally-for-tiktok)
- Platform licences are independent: TikTok-licensed music re-uploaded to YouTube is not covered and will likely be claimed — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/can-i-use-tiktok-music-on-youtube-and-other-platforms-why-you-cant-and-what-to-do-about-it)
- "Royalty-free" describes payment structure, not Content ID status; some royalty-free tracks still trigger claims. Look for tracks that are also explicitly Content-ID-cleared. Free sources include Free Music Archive, Incompetech and Creative Commons libraries (check attribution terms) — [Foxi](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-shorts-copyright-music-library-guide/); [Hello Thematic](https://hellothematic.com/where-youtubers-get-their-music/)

**AI disclosure: YouTube**
- Disclose content that is "meaningfully altered or synthetically generated" and realistic (a viewer could mistake it for a real person, place or event). Examples: a voice clone of a real person, altering real footage of events or places, synthetic performances of a person saying things they didn't say. No disclosure is needed for AI-assisted scripts, titles, outlines, AI thumbnails, standard filters, colour correction or background blur. Disclosure is a toggle in YouTube Studio's upload flow — [YouTube Help: disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) (couldn't fetch directly; details via [ytzolo](https://ytzolo.com/blog/youtube-altered-synthetic-content-policy-2026/) / [Eliro](https://eliro.pro/blog/youtube-ai-content-policy-faceless-creators-2026) summaries)
- Claims of a "January 2026 update" and of 18,000 channel suspensions come only from SEO blogs — [minimatters](https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/) (**unverified**)

**AI disclosure: TikTok**
- Creators must label AI-generated content with realistic images, audio or video. The toggle shows "Creator labeled as AI-generated"; a caption or sticker disclosure is also acceptable. TikTok auto-labels content made with TikTok AI effects or carrying C2PA Content Credentials. Misleading AI content can be suppressed or removed — [TikTok Newsroom: new AI labels](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-labels-for-disclosing-ai-generated-content); [TikTok Newsroom: C2PA partnership](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/partnering-with-our-industry-to-advance-ai-transparency-and-literacy); [TikTok Support: AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label)

**AI disclosure: Meta (Instagram/Facebook)**
- Meta requires people to use its disclosure/label tool when posting organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and may apply penalties otherwise. Labels show as "AI info", applied by detection signals or by self-disclosure — [Meta: approach to labeling AI content (Apr 2024)](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/); [Meta: labeling AI images (Feb 2024)](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/); [Influencer Marketing Hub: AI disclosure by platform](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/)
- The claim that self-disclosure doesn't reduce reach comes from a secondary source — [Junibloom](https://www.junibloom.com/blog/instagram-ai-labels-what-triggers-them) (**unverified against Meta**)

### Inferences
- A simple rule for beginners: if AI made a realistic human face, voice or event, toggle the platform's AI label. If AI only helped you write, caption, or tidy up, no label is needed. AI avatars or cloned voices always get disclosed.
- For faceless AI channels, disclosure alone isn't enough for YouTube monetisation. The inauthentic-content policy also requires meaningful human originality.
