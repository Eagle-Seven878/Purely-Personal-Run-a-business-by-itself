# Platform Specs, Algorithm Signals & Beginner Metrics for Short-Form/Social Video (2025–2026)

> Methodology caveat: This session's network proxy blocked direct page fetches (tiktok.com, about.fb.com, support.google.com, buffer.com, hootsuite, techcrunch, dataslayer). All findings below come from web-search result snippets/summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Official-source claims (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn) were cross-checked against at least one trade outlet (Social Media Today, Tubefilter, MediaPost, TechCrunch) where possible. Numbers from third-party tool vendors (benchmarks) are **industry estimates, not platform-published**, and vary widely between vendors. Treat them as rough guides.

## 1. Per-platform specs: length, aspect ratio/resolution, safe zones, captions, hashtags/SEO, posting frequency

### Takeaway
All five platforms now favour 9:16 vertical at 1080×1920 for short-form. Length ceilings: TikTok 60 min (upload), Instagram Reels 3 min, YouTube Shorts 3 min, Facebook no limit (all videos are now Reels), LinkedIn 15 min (desktop). Discovery now leans on keywords in captions, on-screen text and speech more than hashtags. Instagram caps hashtags at 5. Posting 2–5x/week is a good evidence-based starting cadence.

### Cited Findings

**TikTok**
- Length: 10 min when recorded in-app; up to 60 min when uploaded — [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/tiktok-scheduler/tiktok-video-sizes/); [ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/blog/tiktok-aspect-ratio-complete-guide/)
- Recommended 1080×1920, 9:16; MP4 (H.264 + AAC) is the most reliable format — [PostFast sizes (updated Sept 2026)](https://postfa.st/sizes/tiktok/video); [ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/blog/tiktok-aspect-ratio-complete-guide/)
- Safe zones: TikTok does **not** publish pixel margins. It provides downloadable templates and says the safe zone changes with dimensions, caption length and add-ons. Third-party rule of thumb: keep text/CTAs ~150 px from the top, ~150 px from the right, and ~250–340 px from the bottom (vendors disagree on the exact bottom margin) — [ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/blog/tiktok-aspect-ratio-complete-guide/); [Quso](https://quso.ai/blog/tiktok-dimensions)
- Claim that 9:16 gets "2.3x higher engagement" than 1:1 or 4:5 is a vendor stat with unclear methodology — [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/tiktok-scheduler/tiktok-video-sizes/) (unverified)
- SEO: TikTok analyses keywords in captions, hashtags, on-screen text and spoken audio (which it transcribes). Put the main keyword early in the caption and in on-screen text/voiceover in the first ~3 s. Hashtags matter less than they used to; 3–5 relevant ones is the common advice — [Hootsuite TikTok SEO](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-seo/); [Outfame](https://www.outfame.com/blog/tiktok-seo-2026-how-to-rank-in-search-keywords-captions-hooks); [Dive Media](https://www.divemedia.com.au/marketing-tips-and-insights/social-seo-keywords-vs-hashtags)
- Posting frequency: Buffer analysed 11M+ TikTok uploads from 150k+ accounts. Compared with posting once a week, per-post views rise 17% at 2–5 posts/week, 29% at 6–10/week and 34% at 11+/week. Buffer recommends 2–5/week as the most meaningful step up — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-post-frequency-improves-per-post-reach-report-buffer/802443/)

**Instagram Reels**
- Max length went from 90 s to **3 min** in January 2025 (announced by Mosseri). Third-party analysts say sub-90 s clips still tend to do better for non-follower reach — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/instagram-reels-statistics/); [OneStream](https://onestream.live/blog/how-long-can-instagram-reels-be/)
- **Hashtag cap of 5 per post/Reel** since Dec 19, 2025. Mosseri: "a few specific tags actually perform better than a long list of generic ones." Instagram says ranking leans more on caption keywords, on-screen text and visual content — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/instagram-now-limits-you-to-five-hashtags-per-post/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)
- Posting frequency: Buffer's 2026 data points to 3–5 posts/week — [Buffer via PostEverywhere summary](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media); [Buffer engagement report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- Spec: 1080×1920, 9:16 (same as other vertical platforms) — [PostFast](https://postfa.st/sizes/facebook/reels)

**YouTube Shorts**
- Since Oct 15, 2024, Shorts can be up to **3 minutes** if square or vertical — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)
- View counting changed on **March 31, 2025**: a Shorts "view" now counts every start or replay, with no minimum watch time. The old metric survives as **"engaged views"**, which is still used for YPP eligibility and revenue share. Public view counts from before and after this date are not directly comparable — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-what-creators-need-to-know-about-the-new-metrics/)
- Relevance signals include title, description, transcript and on-screen text in the first seconds (per Creator Insider/Todd Beaupré commentary as summarised by third parties) — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-youtube-algorithm-works); [Lia Haberman](https://liahaberman.substack.com/p/icymi-how-youtubes-2025-algorithm)

**YouTube long-form (basic)**
- Accepts 16:9 standard. Found no new 2025–26 official spec changes in searches. See the metrics section for retention benchmarks.

**Facebook Reels / video**
- In June 2025 Meta began turning **all new Facebook videos into Reels** and removed Reels length and format restrictions (rolled out gradually worldwide). Older guides citing a 90 s max are out of date — [About Meta (June 2025)](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/06/making-it-easier-create-videos-facebook/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-renames-all-videos-reels/750973/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/406751/meta-to-share-all-facebook-videos-as-reels.html)
- Any aspect ratio is accepted, but 9:16 at 1080×1920 is recommended — [SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/how-long-can-a-facebook-reel-be/); [PostFast](https://postfa.st/sizes/facebook/reels)

**LinkedIn video**
- Up to **15 min** from desktop, 10 min from mobile. Aspect ratios 9:16 to 16:9 (16:9, 1:1, 4:5, 9:16). MP4 recommended. Regular posts up to 5 GB (ads 500 MB). Captions can be uploaded as **.SRT** or burned in — [LinkedIn Help: video specs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1311816); [LinkedIn ad specs](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/sponsored-content/video-ads/specs); [Brandwatch](https://www.brandwatch.com/blog/linkedin-video-specs/)
- Vertical 1080×1920 reportedly gets a distribution boost, and LinkedIn has a dedicated full-screen vertical video feed (also on desktop) that reaches non-followers — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-video-presentation-discovery-updates/739214/); [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-video-posts)
- Length: third-party analysis says videos over 3 min earn 1.21x median reach vs 0.95x for 30–60 s clips. Suggested lengths are 30–90 s for single-point clips and 2–5 min for tutorials or case studies — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-video-posts) (vendor data)

### Inferences
- A single master edit at 1080×1920 9:16, with key text kept in the centre ~2/3 of the frame, will fit every platform's safe zone. Keep the bottom ~20% and the right edge clear.
- "Keyword SEO" (spoken + on-screen + caption) now outweighs hashtags on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. Beginners should say the topic out loud and put it on screen in the first 3 seconds.
- 60–90 s is the safe cross-platform length. It is under every short-form ceiling, and longer videos are only worth it when retention holds.

### Gaps
- Could not read official TikTok, YouTube or Instagram help pages directly (proxy blocked). No official pixel safe-zone numbers exist for TikTok, and none were verified for Reels or Shorts.
- No official posting-frequency guidance from any platform was found. Frequency figures come from Buffer only.
- Current YouTube long-form upload specs were not re-verified for 2026.

## 2. Official statements on ranking signals (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Every platform now rewards **viewer satisfaction proxies**: watch time/retention, shares (especially DM "sends"), and originality. All of them actively suppress reposted, watermarked or unmodified third-party content. Instagram (Jan 2025, Mosseri) named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top signals. YouTube stresses satisfaction surveys on top of watch time. TikTok makes unoriginal or watermarked content ineligible for the For You feed.

### Cited Findings

**Instagram (Mosseri)**
- January 2025: Mosseri named **watch time, likes per reach, and sends (DM shares) per reach** as the most important ranking factors. Likes matter slightly more for reaching existing followers. Sends matter more for reaching non-followers via Reels and Explore — [Dataslayer summary (updated June 2026)](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers); [Blck Alpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/instagram-algorithm-2026) (secondary summaries of Mosseri videos)
- Originality: Instagram has prioritised original content since 2022. **April 30, 2026**: the anti-aggregator policy was extended from Reels to photos and carousels. Accounts that mainly post unoriginal content are removed from recommendation surfaces, though they are still shown to existing followers. Reposts that are "significantly changed" are exempt. The approved ways to share others' work are the Repost button or Collab tagging — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414773/instagram-algorithm-update-discourages-reposted-co.html)
- Claims that "75% of US recommendations now come from original posts" and that original creators saw "40–60% reach increases" appear in search summaries of this coverage. The attribution is unclear, so treat these as unverified — [search summary of Tubefilter/ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-restricts-reach-content-aggregators/)
- Earlier (April 2024) ranking update to surface more content from smaller original creators — [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators) (older, 2024)
- Watermarks: Mosseri said Instagram tries "not to recommend Reels with logos from other apps, but if it's your own logo, don't worry about it" — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/) (statement dates from roughly 2023–24; policy since 2021)
- Trial Reels: shown **only to non-followers** at first (reported window ~72 h). They are kept off your grid, hashtag pages and Explore during the trial, and you can share them to followers afterwards. They need a public professional account. Third-party sources give conflicting launch dates and eligibility thresholds (late 2024 vs 2025 rollout; 1,000-follower threshold reported). A reported cap of ~5 trial reels per day and a reported Mosseri comment that trial reels usually get less reach than regular Reels are **unverified** — [PostFast](https://postfa.st/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagram-trial-reels-work-72-hours/); [SocialChamp](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels/)

**Facebook (Meta)**
- **July 14, 2025**: Meta cracked down on unoriginal content. Accounts that repeatedly reuse others' text, photos or video get reduced distribution and lose monetisation. **Adding a watermark or stitching clips together does not count as meaningful enhancement.** Around 500k spammy accounts were actioned and about 10M impersonator profiles removed in 2025 — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/); [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-cracking-down-unoriginal-content-ai-replicas/753001/)
- **March 2026**: Meta published a definition of "original". Content must be filmed or created by the publisher, or "enhanced" with commentary, information, storyline or critique. Watch-alongs, facial-expression reactions, simple stitching or narrating what is already on screen count as unoriginal and are deprioritised in Feed and Reels. Meta says views and time spent on original Reels **doubled** in H2 2025 vs the prior year after it deprioritised unoriginal content — [About Meta (Mar 2026)](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/413530/meta-defines-original-content-tests-content-pro.html)

**TikTok**
- Recommendation works by testing: new videos are shown to a small group, and their engagement decides wider rollout. Watch time and completion are the most heavily weighted signals (as summarised by Hootsuite and others from TikTok's Creator Academy "How TikTok recommends content" page) — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/guidelines-recommendation-system-intro); [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/)
- For You Feed Eligibility Standards: "unoriginal content" means material "imported or copied from other sources without any new, creative edits." Clips showing **someone else's watermark or logo** are the named example, and adding subtitles based only on the original audio does not count as an edit. The penalty is FYF ineligibility (suppressed distribution), not removal. Other low-quality examples given: very short videos, static images, GIF-only videos. Guidelines updated effective Sept 13, 2025 — [TikTok FYF Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards); [ContentIQ summary](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok)

**YouTube**
- YouTube (Todd Beaupré, Creator Insider / Rene Ritchie) describes ranking around **viewer satisfaction**. Signals include post-watch surveys, likes and dislikes, shares, rewatches, skipping and "Not interested", on top of CTR and watch time. It optimises for predicted satisfaction, not raw views — [Buffer YouTube algorithm guide](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-algorithm/); [Lia Haberman](https://liahaberman.substack.com/p/icymi-how-youtubes-2025-algorithm); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-viewer-satisfaction-algorithm-2026/)
- **July 15, 2025**: the YPP "repetitious content" policy was renamed **"inauthentic content"**, covering mass-produced or templated content with minimal variation. YouTube clarified this is **not** a change to the reused-content policy, so posting your own videos from other platforms remains fine — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [YouTube Help monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**LinkedIn**
- Dwell time is heavily weighted. A third-party claim says a >50% video watch is treated like a comment-level signal (not officially confirmed) — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-video-posts)
- LinkedIn reported video watch time **up 36% YoY** (2024 figure). A related stat says video gets 1.4x more engagement than other formats — [Mediabrief](https://mediabrief.com/linkedin-enhances-vertical-video-tools/); [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/linkedin-video-statistics)
- Contradicting view: some analysts report LinkedIn video reach falling — [The State of Brand](https://www.thestateofbrand.com/news/linkedin-killing-video-reach)

### Inferences
- The common formula across platforms: a strong hook to cut early skips, then high retention, then something worth sending to a friend, all in original, unwatermarked footage.
- "Shares/sends per reach" is the metric most worth optimising for growth on Instagram. "New followers per video" is a strong growth proxy on TikTok.

### Gaps
- Could not read primary Mosseri videos or Instagram @creators posts. The Jan 2025 "top 3 signals" claim relies on secondary summaries, although several outlets agree.
- No official TikTok statement for 2025–26 with numeric completion thresholds. Claims like "70% completion needed" are vendor folklore.
- LinkedIn has no official public statement ranking video signals.

## 3. Hook retention, average view duration, muted viewing, captions

### Takeaway
The first ~3 seconds decide distribution. Instagram now measures this directly with "skip rate", and YouTube with "viewed vs swiped away". Strong short-form retention is roughly 70%+ average percentage viewed. Long-form "good" is ~40–55%. The widely quoted "85% of video watched muted" figure is a **2016 Facebook-publisher statistic**, not current platform data. TikTok is a sound-on platform. Captions still help accessibility and reportedly lift view time.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok for Business: 63% of the highest-CTR videos/ads deliver their key message within the first 3 seconds (a TikTok ad-research stat, originally ~2021–22, i.e. older) — [HeyOrca](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/best-tiktok-hooks); [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/)
- Instagram added **skip rate** (the % of viewers who scroll past within the first 3 seconds) to Reels Insights in **August 2025**. Instagram publishes no official benchmark. Industry guidance: under ~30–40% is healthy, over ~50% means the hook is failing — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/); [Babbleboxx](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next); [Inro](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-insights-update-2025)
- YouTube Shorts "Viewed vs swiped away" (VVSA): a study of 3.3B Shorts views found 70–90% viewed performed best and under 60% performed poorly. Some creators target 75–80% — [Medium (Lacombled study)](https://medium.com/@antoinelacombled/cracking-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-study-of-3-3-billion-views-4711fdf7931b); [Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/how-youtube-shorts-algorithm-works/); YouTube's own explainer: [YouTube Community video](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-video/273390203/new-youtube-shorts-metric-viewed-vs-swiped-away?hl=en)
- Short-form average % viewed/completion (vendor estimates, which vary): YouTube Shorts ~73%, TikTok ~78%, Reels ~65%. Another source gives TikTok in-feed (21–34 s) a median of 62% and Meta Reels (15–30 s) a median of 50%. Each extra 15 s of length costs ~15–20 points of completion on skippable formats — [Socialinsider video metrics](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/video-metrics/); [AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/glossary/metrics/video-completion-rate-vcr) (**conflicting vendor numbers; use as ranges**)
- YouTube Shorts target: at least 60% average retention, 70%+ excellent — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention); [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks)
- YouTube long-form: YouTube publishes **no official retention benchmark**. Third-party guides give ~40–55% average percentage viewed as competitive and ~30–45% as healthy for 15–30 min videos. Some cite ~60% of viewers still watching at 30 s as a good intro — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/); [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-benchmarks); [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention)
- TikTok: a drop of more than 30% in the first 3 seconds of the retention graph signals a failing opening frame (vendor guidance) — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/tiktok-viral-retention-rate-2026)
- Muted viewing: "85% of Facebook video is watched without sound" comes from **Digiday, 2016**, based on publisher reports (e.g. Mic). Facebook never officially confirmed it — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/publishers-say-85-percent-of-facebook-video-is-watched-without-sound/); [SousTitreur critique](https://st4.ca/blog/85-really-facebook-without-sound) (**OLD; do not present as current**)
- Captions reportedly increase average view time by ~12% (older Facebook-era stat; original source unclear) — [Instapage](https://instapage.com/blog/closed-captioning-mute-videos)

### Inferences
- For beginners: put the payoff or visual promise in the first 1–2 s, with on-screen text restating it. Always add captions or on-screen text, because feed contexts (Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram) are often watched muted. TikTok and Reels are more sound-on, but captions still add keyword SEO value.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) platform-published data on the share of muted viewing was found. The often-cited "69% watch muted in public" (Verizon Media/Publicis, 2019) could not be verified in this session.
- No official hook-retention benchmarks were published by any platform.

## 4. Key metrics for beginners, what "good" looks like, and where to find them

### Takeaway
Track four core numbers per video: (1) **hook metric** (IG skip rate / YT viewed-vs-swiped / TikTok 3-s retention drop), (2) **average % viewed / completion**, (3) **shares/sends per view**, (4) **follows per view**. Compare against your own median, since vendor benchmarks conflict widely.

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok** (TikTok Studio → Analytics → Content → select a video): total views, likes, comments, shares, **average watch time**, **% who watched the full video**, traffic source, and a retention graph under audience insights. **New followers** per video is a key growth indicator: 100K views that bring 10K followers beat 1M views that bring zero — [Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-analytics/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-metrics/); [InfluenceFlow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/tiktok-creator-metrics-the-complete-2026-guide-to-tracking-analyzing-optimizing-your-performance/)
- **Instagram** (Professional dashboard → Insights → select a Reel): views, watch time, average watch time, **skip rate** (added Aug 2025, replacing view rate), retention graph, shares/sends, saves, follows — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/); [Sociality](https://sociality.io/blog/instagram-reels-analytics/)
- **YouTube Shorts** (YouTube Studio → Analytics / Content → select a Short): **Viewed vs swiped away** (aim for 70%+, worry below 60%), average percentage viewed (60%+ good, 70%+ excellent), engaged views vs views (since Mar 31, 2025), subscribers gained — [Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/how-youtube-shorts-algorithm-works/); [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-explained); [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-what-creators-need-to-know-about-the-new-metrics/)
- **YouTube long-form**: impressions CTR, average view duration, average percentage viewed (~40–55% competitive), 30-second retention — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/); [Humble & Brag new-channel benchmarks](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/new-youtube-channel-average-views)
- Engagement rate benchmarks depend heavily on the denominator. Quid's 2026 median for brands is 2.01% against followers, and Socialinsider's is 4.20% against views on TikTok. Nano accounts (under 10K) average ~8–15% — [Socialinsider TikTok benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok); [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok); [InfluenceFlow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/tiktok-creator-metrics-the-complete-2026-guide-to-tracking-analyzing-optimizing-your-performance/)
- Vendor claim that TikTok needs "70%+ completion for viral distribution (up from 50% in 2024)" is unverified folklore, not a TikTok statement — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/tiktok-viral-retention-rate-2026)
- Buffer's 2026 engagement report (52M+ posts) gives cross-platform engagement baselines — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/) (could not open for specific figures)

### Inferences
- Proposed beginner "good" targets (a synthesis of the vendor ranges above, not official): short-form hook pass rate (1 − skip rate, or VVSA) ≥ 65–70%; average % viewed ≥ 60% for videos under 60 s; shares ≥ ~1% of views; follows ≥ ~0.5–1% of views. Judge trends over 10–20 posts, not single videos.
- Because the YouTube view definition changed in March 2025, beginners should compare **engaged views** when benchmarking against older data.

### Gaps
- No platform-official "good" thresholds for shares per view or follows per view. The suggestions above are inference.
- LinkedIn and Facebook video analytics menus were not researched in detail. Facebook's Professional Dashboard and LinkedIn post analytics are known to show views, watch time and audience, but this was not verified in this session.

## 5. Cross-posting the same video everywhere; watermark issues

### Takeaway
Cross-posting **your own original** video to every platform is fine and widely practised. The penalties target **other apps' watermarks/logos** and **unoriginal reposts of other people's content**. Export a clean, watermark-free master (not a TikTok download) and tailor the caption, keywords and cover per platform.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram: it avoids recommending Reels "with logos from other apps", but your own logo is fine — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)
- Instagram in 2025 was reported to pull "recycled content with watermarks or low production value" from recommendations — [Cut.Pro summary](https://cut.pro/en/blog/instagram-buries-watermarked-reposts-2026) (secondary)
- TikTok: clips with someone else's watermark or logo are the named example of unoriginal, FYF-ineligible content — [TikTok FYF Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards)
- Facebook: adding a watermark is **not** a meaningful enhancement of others' content. Habitual reposting leads to reduced reach and demonetisation — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/); [About Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)
- YouTube: the July 2025 inauthentic-content update explicitly does **not** stop you posting content from other platforms or reposting videos. Such content remains monetisation-eligible (as long as it is your own or transformative) — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)

### Inferences
- Beginner workflow: edit in a neutral editor (CapCut and similar can export without the watermark), export 1080×1920 without platform branding, upload natively to each app, and rewrite the caption and keywords per platform. Instagram's native Reels → Facebook sharing is acceptable.
- Using platform-native sounds (TikTok or IG trending audio) cannot be cross-posted as-is. Add the audio inside each app (inference; not verified in sources).

### Gaps
- No platform has officially stated whether identical uploads of the same original video across platforms receive any "duplicate" demotion. Available evidence suggests they do not, provided there is no foreign watermark, but this is not confirmed.
