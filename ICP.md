## THE ONE READER

A Filipino SME owner or coach. Already earning real revenue, not yet systemised. Profitable on the
surface, but cannot name their breakeven, their net income percentage, or their true cost per
employee. So they keep hustling to cover for not knowing the figures. Every post is written to this
one person, in the second person, as if Gilbert is speaking to her directly. Not "founders." Not
"entrepreneurs." Her.

## REGISTER SPLIT — choose one per post, never blur them

The ICP is two readers who share one problem. Pick the register before writing and keep the nouns
consistent through the whole post.

- **SME owner.** Speaks in stock, suppliers, payroll, orders, capital, "the business." Scenes: the
  warehouse, the purchase order, the loan, the staff count, the supplier credit line. The pain shows
  up as cash that moves but never settles, and a big order taken without knowing what it leaves.
- **Coach.** Speaks in clients, launches, programs, "my offer," personal brand, cohorts. Scenes:
  the funnel, the package price, the cost per delivery, the team hired before the system. The pain
  shows up as revenue with no idea of the cost to deliver, and borrowing to launch before there is a
  product.

Same conviction (numbers before hustle), different vocabulary. A post that mixes warehouse language
with launch language sounds like neither reader.

## THE CORE PROBLEM (in their own words — use these lines)

- *"I'm grinding and hustling but I don't know my breakeven."*
- *"Money comes in, but I can't tell my net income percentage."*
- *"I borrow or spend more than I need because I'm not sure of my figures."*

**Why they haven't solved it yet:** they were taught to admire the hustle, not to read the numbers.
They have been sold "ecosystems" and onboarding before they were sold clarity. They equate effort
with progress, so they grind harder instead of stopping to find the one number that would let them
decide instead of guess.

---

## WHAT THEY WANT INSTEAD

- **Tangible:** to know their breakeven, net income percentage, and real cost per employee before
  they spend, borrow, or scale. To take an order and know, that day, what it leaves them.
- **Emotional:** peace of mind. To stop feeling like the grind is the only proof they are committed.
  To stop being quietly afraid that they do not actually know their own business.
- **Identity:** an owner who decides on purpose, who can get out of the rat race, who can circulate
  what they build to more people.

## WHAT FRUSTRATES THEM ABOUT OTHER OPTIONS

| Frustration | Why it matters |
|-------------|----------------|
| "I've been burned by programs that promised an ecosystem and disappeared." | Their top objection. Trust is the gate. Earn it with a real scene and a real figure, never hype. |
| Gurus who sell onboarding then vanish | They have paid for motion, not clarity. They are tired of it. |
| "Conglomerate" cosplayers who can't state their net income | They sense the pretending and resent it, even when they cannot name why. |

## TRUST FACTORS (what makes them believe Gilbert)

A lived scene from real poverty and real business, not a credential. An exact peso figure attached
to a real outcome. A principle that costs him something on the page. Naming the manipulation in the
market plainly. Pointing to the reader's gain, his family's gain, the less fortunate, never to his
own greatness.

---

## CONTENT DIRECTION FROM THIS ICP

- Topics that resonate: knowing your breakeven · net income percentage · cost per employee · when
  NOT to borrow · why the grind hides the gap · cash flow as freedom · mutual-win over scarcity.
- Tone that connects: reflective, direct, dignified. Speaks to her like a brother who has been
  poorer than she has and read more financial statements than she has.
- Proof she needs: a real figure, a real client situation (unnamed), a real decision.
- What to avoid saying: hype · cure-claims · "act now" · "crush the competition" · "just hustle
  harder" · generic LinkedIn-growth advice · anything that sounds like the people who burned her.

---

## EXTRA PAIN LINE (their words)

- *"I make money but I never know what is left."*

**Decision-maker note:** the founder/owner decides, but a spouse or co-owner is often part of a yes.

**Buying trigger:** watching a peer they consider an equal scale calmly while they still grind blind;
or a loan, expansion, or big order that suddenly forces the numbers into the open.

## ROOT FEAR (Level 3 of pain excavation)

Working this hard while secretly not knowing whether the business is actually profitable — and the
fear of being exposed, by a lender, a partner, or themselves, as not knowing their own numbers.

> Do NOT use Gilbert's personal "user" wound or his down-season story as the prospect's pain. That is
> his story. The prospect's fear is being exposed as not knowing their own figures.

---

## ICP FIT SIGNALS

**They FIT if:** earning real revenue · cannot name breakeven or net income % · facing a
spend/borrow/scale decision · open to looking at their figures.

**They DON'T FIT (yet) if:** pre-revenue · only want hype and a quick win · cannot name a single
number and show no real pain · looking for someone to "crush the competition" for them.

---

## TOP OBJECTION (the default for this ICP)

"I've been burned before. I paid for a program that promised an ecosystem and then they were gone."
Handle this Trust objection FIRST. Most of these owners have already been burned once.

---

## COMMUNICATION STYLE TO READ ON THE CALL

Some are direct drivers — match with directness. Some are relationship-first — match with warmth and
patience. Many are ashamed they cannot answer for their own numbers. Create a safe space before
asking a single number question.
