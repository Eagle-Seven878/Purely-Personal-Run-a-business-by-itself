# Session 3 · RISK & DEFENSE — Facilitator Script

**Format** Live · 120 minutes · pods of 8
**Entry gate** Session 2 pass — Position Card, open position at ≤30% LTV
**Pass gate** **Risk Card** + passes the margin-call drill unaided
**Slides** [`SLIDES.md`](SLIDES.md)

**New rung.** The source curriculum taught participants to open a leveraged
position and stopped. This is the session that teaches them to survive one. It is
the most important rung in the ladder and the one that protects the programme's
reputation, because a drawdown is when people get hurt, and when complaints,
refunds and reputational damage arrive.

---

## BLOCK A · OPEN — 5 min · slides 1–2

Read the standing disclosure.

> Last session you learned to open a position. Anyone can open a position — the
> platform makes it four taps and it *wants* you to.
>
> Tonight you learn the part nobody teaches, because it isn't exciting and it
> doesn't sell: **how to still be holding this in two years.**
>
> Cards out. Read me your liquidation price, one per pod.

## MODULE 1 · THE THREE NUMBERS — 20 min

### B1 · Your numbers, not the platform's — 6 min · slides 3–4

> Three numbers run your position. Two belong to the platform. One belongs to you.
>
> **85% — margin call.** The platform's warning line.
> **91% — liquidation.** The platform's action line.
> **30% — your ceiling.** Yours. Nobody enforces it but you.
>
> Notice the gap between 30 and 85. That gap is not wasted capacity — **that gap is
> the entire product.** It's what you're actually buying with the discipline.

### B2 · The drawdown table — 8 min · slide 5

*Work the table live. Have them compute their own row.*

> `drawdown to liquidation = 1 − (your LTV ÷ 0.91)`
>
> Calculators. Everyone compute yours.

| Opening LTV | Margin call | Liquidated | Survives −53% (2021)? | Survives −77% (2022)? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 78% | −8.2% | −14.3% | No | No |
| 50% | −41.2% | −45.1% | No | No |
| 30% | −64.7% | −67.0% | Yes | No |
| 20% | −76.5% | −78.0% | Yes | Barely |

> Now look at the 30% row honestly. **30% does not survive 2022.** Your ceiling is
> not a guarantee — it's a much better bet than 50%, and it is still a bet.
>
> That's why the ceiling alone isn't the answer. The reserve is the other half, and
> it's the half that turns a −77% year from a wipeout into an opportunity.

### B3 · What a margin call actually feels like — 6 min · slide 6

> Here's what nobody warns you about. A margin call does not arrive on a calm
> Tuesday afternoon while you're at your desk.
>
> It arrives at 3am. It arrives during a week when everything is red and every
> feed you follow says it's going lower. It arrives when your business is also
> having a bad month, because these things correlate.
>
> You will not be making a good decision in that moment. **So you make the decision
> now, tonight, in this room, while nothing is wrong.** That's what the rest of
> this session is.

## MODULE 2 · THE RESERVE DOCTRINE — 20 min

### C1 · The rule — 5 min · slide 7

> **For every peso you deploy, you hold a peso back.**
>
> Reserve is not "money I haven't invested yet." It is a **funded position in
> doing nothing**, and it has a job: it is the only thing that saves you in a
> margin call.

### C2 · Where reserve lives — 6 min · slide 8

> Three rules for where it sits.
>
> **One — not in the position.** Obvious, but people drift. Reserve deployed is
> reserve that doesn't exist.
>
> **Two — not in the same asset.** Bitcoin held as reserve against a Bitcoin
> position is not reserve. When you need it, it has fallen by the same percentage
> that created the emergency. Reserve is stablecoin or pesos.
>
> **Three — reachable in under an hour.** Reserve in a time-locked earn product
> with a seven-day unbonding is not reserve. Check the lock terms on whatever you
> put it in. Do it tonight.

### C3 · Programme LTV — 5 min · slide 9

> Two numbers, and you need both.
>
> **Position LTV** — what Binance shows. Loan ÷ collateral.
> **Programme LTV** — loan ÷ (deployed **+** reserve). What you actually carry.
>
> $100 deployed, $30 borrowed, $100 reserve → position LTV 30%, programme LTV 15%.
>
> Compute both. Write both on the Risk Card.

### C4 · The rebuild rule — 4 min · slide 10

> And when you *use* the reserve — because one day you will — **rebuilding it is
> the next thing you do.** Before a new position. Before adding to an existing one.
> Before anything.
>
> A participant running without reserve is not running BBT. They're running
> leverage and hoping.

## MODULE 3 · ALARM ARCHITECTURE — 20 min

### D1 · Four alarms — 8 min · slides 11–12 · **[LIVE]**

*Demonstrate setting price alarms on the platform, then have every pod set all
four before moving on.*

> Four alarms. Everyone sets all four tonight.
>
> **Alarm 1 — LTV 40%.** Awareness. You do nothing. You just know.
> **Alarm 2 — LTV 50%.** Prepare. Reserve confirmed reachable. Top-up amount
> calculated. Not executed — calculated.
> **Alarm 3 — LTV 60%.** Act. Top up from reserve to bring LTV back to 30%.
> **Alarm 4 — liquidation price × 1.3.** The "this is serious" line.
>
> Note that alarm 3 fires at 60%, which is **25 points below the platform's margin
> call.** You are never going to meet Binance's margin call, because you'll have
> acted long before it. That's the design.

### D2 · Alarms are price, not LTV — 5 min · slide 13

> One practical thing. Alarms are usually set on **price**, not LTV. So convert:
>
> `price at target LTV = entry × (your entry LTV ÷ target LTV)`
>
> Entry $100,000 at 30% LTV, want an alarm at 50% LTV:
> 100,000 × (0.30 ÷ 0.50) = **$60,000.**
>
> Everyone compute your four prices now. Write them on the card.

### D3 · The decision tree — 7 min · slide 14

> When an alarm fires you do not think. You read.
>
> **LTV 40%** → nothing. Log the date.
> **LTV 50%** → confirm reserve is reachable. Calculate the top-up. Do not execute.
> **LTV 60%** → execute the top-up from reserve. Return LTV to 30%. Message your
> coach the same day.
> **Reserve exhausted and LTV still climbing** → repay the loan from reserve,
> close the borrow, keep the Bitcoin. **Losing the loan is not losing.** Losing
> the Bitcoin is losing.
>
> That last line is the one to remember. The strategy is optional. The asset is
> the point.

## MODULE 4 · THE MARGIN-CALL DRILL — 35 min · **[GATE]**

*This is the pass gate. Run it like a fire drill: unannounced pace, no notes for
the final round, coaches score individually.*

### E1 · Round 1, worked together — 8 min · slide 16

> Your position: entry **$100,000**, $100 deployed, borrowed to **30%**, so $30
> owed. Reserve **$100**.
>
> Bitcoin falls to **$55,000.** Calculators.
>
> The drop: 45%. New LTV: 30 ÷ 0.55 = **54.5%.**
>
> Which alarm fired? **Alarm 2 — 50%.** What do you do?

*Take answers. Steer:*
> Confirm reserve. Calculate the top-up. **Do not execute.** Alarm 2 is prepare,
> not act.
>
> How much *would* the top-up be to get back to 30%? You need collateral of
> 30 ÷ 0.30 = $100. You have $55. So **$45 from reserve.** Calculated. Not spent.

### E2 · Round 2, in pods — 10 min · slide 17 · **[POD]**

> Same position. Bitcoin falls to **$45,000.**
>
> New LTV. Which alarm. What action. How much. Pods — three minutes.

*Answer: drop 55%, LTV 30 ÷ 0.45 = **66.7%**. Alarm 3. Execute. Collateral needed
$100, have $45, **top up $55 from reserve**, leaving $45 reserve. Rebuild is now
the next priority.*

### E3 · Round 3, the hard one — 10 min · slide 18 · **[POD]**

> You took the round-2 action. You topped up $55 from reserve, so you now hold
> **0.002222 BTC** as collateral, your loan is still **$30**, and your reserve is
> down to **$45**.
>
> Bitcoin keeps falling. **$28,000.**
>
> New LTV. What do you do? Pods — four minutes.

*Answer: collateral = 0.002222 × 28,000 = **$62.22**. LTV = 30 ÷ 62.22 = **48.2%**.
Alarm 2 — calculate, don't execute.*

*Now the real question, and it is a judgement call rather than arithmetic. A
top-up back to 30% needs collateral of $100. They have $62.22, so it costs
**$37.78** of a $45 reserve — leaving **$7.22**. Technically available. Strategically
finished: they'd hold a leveraged position into a falling market with no defence
left.*

*The better move: **repay the $30 loan outright from the $45 reserve.** The loan
closes. They keep all 0.002222 BTC, unencumbered, with **$15** still in reserve.
Liquidation risk goes to zero.*

> Notice what just happened. You started with 0.001 BTC. You are ending this
> drawdown with **0.002222 BTC** — more than double — with no loan and money left
> over. And Bitcoin is down 72%.
>
> That's the whole programme in one drill.
>
> The lesson is not "top up until the reserve is gone." It's this: **there is a
> point where the right move is to close the loan and keep the asset.** Losing the
> loan is not losing. Losing the Bitcoin is losing.
>
> The exit isn't defeat. It's the plan.

### E4 · Round 4, individual and scored — 7 min · **[GATE]**

*Coaches issue a scenario to each participant individually. No notes. Participant
states: new LTV, which alarm, action, amount, remaining reserve. Coach scores
pass/fail on the Risk Card.*

**A participant who cannot do this unaided does not enter Session 4. They repeat
Session 3 with the next cohort — with their position open and their coach
watching.**
