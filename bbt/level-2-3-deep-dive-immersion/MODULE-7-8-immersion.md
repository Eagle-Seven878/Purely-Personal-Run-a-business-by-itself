# MODULES 7 & 8 · THE PLAYBOOK AND THE TRANSFER — **IMMERSION ONLY**

**Level 3 · 270 minutes · Day 4 · face to face only**
**Slides** [`SLIDES-7-8.md`](SLIDES-7-8.md)

These two modules are what the ₱75,000 buys over the ₱15,000, and they are
face-to-face only for a reason that isn't commercial: **a playbook built alone at
a laptop is a form. A playbook built with someone asking "why that number?" is a
decision.** You cannot get that over a screen-share with eight other people.

# MODULE 7 · YOUR PERSONAL PLAYBOOK — 180 min

## PART 1 · WHY WRITTEN — 20 min · **[LAYER A]**

### A1 · Who the playbook is for — 10 min · slide 1

> Everything this week has been rules. Today you write **yours.**
>
> And I want to be precise about who this document is for, because it changes how
> you write it.
>
> **It is not for me. It is not for your family. It is for you, at 3am, eighteen
> months from now, tired and frightened and holding a phone.**
>
> That person is not as clever as you are right now. They've had bad sleep and
> two months of red candles and someone in a group chat telling them it's going
> to zero. They will not re-derive anything. They will do what the paper says, if
> the paper is clear, and something stupid if it isn't.
>
> Write for that person. Short sentences. Numbers, not principles.

### A2 · The test for every line — 10 min · slide 2 · **[LAYER A]**

> Every line in your playbook has to pass one test:
>
> **Could a tired person follow this at 3am without deciding anything?**
>
> "Manage risk appropriately" — fails. Nothing to do.
> "Keep LTV low" — fails. How low, measured when, and then what?
> "If LTV reaches 60%, transfer ₱____ from reserve, or repay the loan in full" —
> passes. There's an action and a number.
>
> Hold every line to that. Faculty will hold you to it this afternoon.

## PART 2 · BUILDING IT — 120 min · **[FACULTY IN THE ROOM]**

*Six blocks, twenty minutes each. Participants write; faculty circulate and
challenge. The challenge is the product — every number gets asked about once.*

### B1 · Your position — 20 min · slide 3
Entry, deployed, borrowed, LTV, liquidation price, reserve. Carried from the
Position Card, now in their own document.
**Faculty challenge:** *"Your liquidation price — say it without looking."*

### B2 · Your three buckets — 20 min · slide 4
Reserve, dry powder, carry. Amounts, and where each physically sits.
**Faculty challenge:** *"Show me where the reserve is. Which account? How long to
reach it? Is it locked?"* Reserve in a seven-day unbonding product is not reserve
and most people discover this here.

### B3 · Your ladder — 20 min · slide 5
Three levels from the Level Card, three deploy amounts, weighted to the deepest.
**Faculty challenge:** *"Price is at your 0.618 tomorrow. How much, exactly?"*

### B4 · Your alarms — 20 min · slide 6
Four prices, converted from LTV. Set on the phone in the room.
**Faculty challenge:** *"Alarm 3 fires while you're in a client meeting. What
happens?"* If the answer is "I'd deal with it later", the plan has a hole.

### B5 · Your growth ceiling — 20 min · slide 7 · **[LAYER A]**
**The block most participants have never considered.**

> A number at which you stop adding. Not a target — a **ceiling.**
>
> Here's how you find yours. Ask: at what size would this position becoming
> worthless change my life? Not annoy me. **Change it.** School fees, the
> business, the house.
>
> Whatever that number is — your ceiling is below it. Write it down today, while
> you're calm, because you will not be able to set it honestly during a bull run.
> That's the entire point of setting it now.

**Faculty challenge:** *"Say the number out loud."* People who won't say it
haven't chosen it.

### B6 · Your never-list — 20 min · slide 8
Three things, in their own handwriting, that they will never do.
**Faculty challenge:** *"Which of these have you already been tempted by this
week?"* The honest answer usually names the one that matters.

## PART 3 · THE READ-BACK — 40 min · **[GATE]** · slide 9

*Each participant reads their playbook aloud to one faculty member and one peer.
The peer's only job is to ask "what would you actually do?" at any line that
doesn't say.*

> You'll read yours out loud. Not to be graded — because **you cannot hear a
> vague sentence until you say it.** Every one of you will find at least one line
> that sounded fine written down and sounds like nothing spoken.
>
> Fix those lines now, while faculty is in the room.

**Faculty signs each playbook.** An unsigned playbook is not a Level 3 pass.

# MODULE 8 · THE TRANSFER QUESTION — 90 min

## PART 1 · THE THIRD WORD — 25 min · **[LAYER A]**

### C1 · What "Die" meant — 12 min · slides 10–11

> Buy, Borrow, **Die.** That's the original name of what you've learned. We call
> ours Thrive, and the third word isn't marketing — it's mechanical.
>
> The classical version: buy an asset, borrow against it instead of selling, and
> when you die it passes to your heirs. You never sold, so the sale never
> happened.
>
> The strategy only **completes** if the asset transfers.
>
> Now — land transfers whether you plan for it or not. There's a title, a
> registry, lawyers, and the state knows the asset exists.
>
> **Bitcoin secured by credentials only you hold transfers to nobody.**
>
> If your family cannot reach that account, the strategy doesn't complete. It
> just ends. Ten years of discipline becomes a number nobody can get to.

### C2 · Why Thrive — 13 min · slide 12

> We call it Thrive because there's a version that doesn't require you to die
> first.
>
> The asset stays. Borrowing capacity grows as it appreciates. At some point the
> position can fund things — a business, a house, an education — without being
> sold. That's thriving **on** an asset instead of consuming it.
>
> Same mechanic, better ending. But it still depends on the transfer question,
> because a strategy measured in decades has to survive you.

## PART 2 · THE FOUR THINGS — 45 min · **[WRITTEN IN THE ROOM]** · slides 13–16

### D1 · An access document — 12 min
Where the accounts are, which platforms, what the recovery process is.
**Not passwords in a file.** A written record of *what exists and how it is
reached.* Drafted here, finished at home this week.

### D2 · One person who knows — 10 min
> A strategy nobody knows about is a strategy that dies with you. One trusted
> person, minimum. Name them today. Write the name down.

### D3 · The loan is a liability your heirs inherit — 13 min · **[LAYER A]**
**This surprises everybody. Give it room.**

> If you die with a borrow open, the platform still holds its claim. Your family
> inherits **collateral and a loan.** If nobody services it, it liquidates — and
> the people it liquidates on are the people you were doing this for.
>
> So write the instruction: if I'm not here, **repay the loan from reserve first,
> then hold.** One sentence. It's the sentence that keeps your family from being
> force-sold at the worst possible moment.

### D4 · Proper advice — 10 min
> Philippine estate treatment of digital assets is genuinely unsettled. I'm
> faculty, not your lawyer, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.
>
> Get proper advice. What I can tell you is that doing nothing is also a decision,
> and it's the one that loses everything.

## PART 3 · THE QUESTION — 20 min · slide 17

> One question, and then you're done with the Immersion.
>
> **If you died tonight, could your family reach this?**

*Silence. Do not fill it. Let it sit for a full thirty seconds.*

> If the answer is no, you don't have a strategy. You have a hobby with money
> in it.
>
> You have four boxes on your playbook. Nobody leaves this room having ticked
> them all, and that's honest — they take weeks. But you leave knowing they exist,
> which puts you ahead of almost everyone holding this asset.
