# MODULE 1 · CHART STRUCTURE & LEVELS

**Level 2 & 3 · 180 minutes · Day 1 · both modes**
**Slides** [`SLIDES-1.md`](SLIDES-1.md)

The prospectus promises "chart structure, level-setting on weekly timeframes."
This is that module. It comes first because everything downstream — where you
buy, where your alarms sit, where your ladder deploys — is a level, and a
participant who cannot set one is guessing with a rulebook.

**[LAYER A]** transfers to any future asset programme. **[LAYER B]** is
crypto-specific — see [`../_shared/layer-model.md`](../_shared/layer-model.md).

## PART 1 · WHY WEEKLY — 30 min · **[LAYER A]**

### A1 · The timeframe decides the person you become — 12 min · slides 1–3

> Before any line goes on a chart, one decision, and it matters more than any
> level you'll draw.
>
> **Which timeframe do you look at?**
>
> Because the timeframe doesn't just change what you see. It changes what kind of
> investor you are, and how often you'll be tempted to do something.
>
> On a **5-minute chart** you'll find a reason to act several times an hour.
> On a **daily chart**, a few times a week.
> On a **weekly chart**, a few times a year.
>
> We use the **weekly**. Not because it's more accurate — it isn't. Because our
> strategy holds for years, and a chart that tempts you daily will eventually win.
>
> Type in the chat: how many times in the last month did you check a price and
> then do nothing? That's the tax the wrong timeframe charges.

### A2 · Structure before indicators — 10 min · slide 4

> We use no indicators in this programme. No RSI, no MACD, nothing that lags.
>
> **Structure only:** where price has turned before, and where it hasn't.
>
> That's not minimalism for its own sake. Every indicator is derived from price.
> If you can read structure, the indicator adds a second opinion from the same
> witness — and a reason to hesitate when you should act.

### A3 · What a level is and isn't — 8 min · slide 5 · **[LAYER A]**

> A level is **a price where a decision has been made before.** Where buying
> overwhelmed selling, or the reverse.
>
> A level is **not a prediction.** It doesn't tell you what will happen. It tells
> you where you'll act if it does. Enormous difference, and it's the one most
> people never make.
>
> Write it: **levels are for deciding in advance, not for knowing in advance.**

## PART 2 · PLOTTING THE SWING — 45 min · **[LAYER A]**

### B1 · Find the swing — 15 min · slides 6–8

> Every Fibonacci you'll ever draw needs two points: a **swing high** and a
> **swing low**. Get those wrong and everything after is decoration.
>
> On the weekly, a swing high is the highest candle with lower candles either side
> of it — a clear turn, not a wobble. Same in reverse for the low.
>
> Rules that keep you honest:
> **One.** Use the most recent complete major move. Not the prettiest one.
> **Two.** If you have to squint to see the swing, it isn't one.
> **Three.** Wicks or bodies — pick one and never change. We use **wicks**, because
> the wick is where trades actually happened.

*[BOTH MODES] Everyone plots the same swing on their own screen. Coaches check
every one before moving on. Face to face: on the wall first, then their own.*

### B2 · Draw the retracement — 15 min · slides 9–10

> Now the retracement from the low up to the high. Three levels matter:
>
> **0.618 · first level** — the shallowest. Most often reached, so it's where
> you'll act most often, and it commits the least.
> **0.50 · second level** — the midpoint.
> **0.382 · third level** — the deepest. Least often reached, and the best price
> when it is.
>
> Notice the order. First, second, third — by how likely you are to see them, not
> by depth. That's the order they'll arrive in and the order you'll deploy in.

### B3 · Your levels are not my levels — 15 min · slide 11 · **[LAYER A]**

> Now something no one else will tell you.
>
> Plot the same chart in this room and we will not all get identical numbers. A
> few percent apart, easily. Different swings chosen, wicks versus bodies,
> different starting points.
>
> **That is fine, and here's why.** We're not trying to find the one true level.
> We're building a **band you decided on in advance.** A level three percent off
> mine still does its job, which is to stop you buying on a feeling.
>
> The precision that matters isn't in the number. It's in **having written it
> down before the candle got there.**
>
> Anyone selling you exact levels is selling certainty, and we don't have any.

## PART 3 · LEVELS INTO DECISIONS — 60 min

### C1 · The three jobs a level does — 12 min · slide 12 · **[LAYER A]**

> A level does three jobs in this programme. Most courses teach the first and
> stop.
>
> **One — where you buy.** The obvious one.
> **Two — where you deploy your ladder.** Module 6. You don't spend everything at
> one level.
> **Three — where your alarms sit.** Module 4. Your LTV alarms convert to prices,
> and those prices live on this chart.
>
> So a level isn't a trading tool here. It's the **shared coordinate system** for
> everything else you'll do.

### C2 · Plotting your own — 25 min · **[POD]** · slide 13

*Every participant plots the current weekly structure on their own screen and
writes their three levels on the Level Card. Coaches check the swing choice
first — a wrong swing makes correct arithmetic worthless.*

**[IMMERSION]** Faculty walks the room. For each participant, ask once: *"why
that swing and not the one before it?"* The answer, not the level, is the
learning.

### C3 · When price is between levels — 12 min · slide 14 · **[LAYER A]**

> What do you do when price is sitting between two of your levels — not at any of
> them?
>
> **Nothing.** That's the answer and it's the hardest one in the programme.
>
> Most of the time, price is between levels. Most of the time, the correct action
> is none. If you need to be doing something to feel like an investor, this
> strategy will be uncomfortable and you should know that now rather than in
> month four.

### C4 · When it goes below every level — 11 min · slide 15 · **[LAYER A]**

> And below the third level? Below 0.382, below everything you planned for?
>
> It happens, and it happens in the years that matter most. 2022 went far below
> any reasonable ladder.
>
> Two things are true. **One:** your ladder is spent, and that's the ladder
> working as designed, not failing. **Two:** your reserve is not for this. Reserve
> defends the position you have. We'll be strict about that in Module 6 because
> it's the most common way a disciplined person blows up.
>
> Below your levels, only **new money from income** buys. Not reserve, not
> borrowed capital.

## PART 4 · RE-PLOTTING — 30 min

### D1 · When to move your levels — 12 min · slide 16 · **[LAYER A]**

> When do you redraw?
>
> **A new swing high or low completes on the weekly.** That's it. That's the only
> trigger.
>
> Not when price goes somewhere you don't like. Not when someone posts a chart.
> Not when you're bored. **A completed weekly swing, or nothing.**
>
> In practice that's a handful of times a year, which is why the quarterly review
> in Module 6 is where re-plotting lives.

### D2 · The dishonest move, named — 10 min · slide 17

> There's a version of re-plotting that is cheating, and I want you to be able to
> catch yourself doing it.
>
> You buy without a signal. Then you move your Fibonacci so that 0.618 lands on
> the price you happened to pay.
>
> **Walang basehan yun.** You've drawn a level around a decision you already made
> and given it the appearance of a plan.
>
> It has exactly one legitimate use: showing you your next levels **given** you
> already bought. It does not make a bad entry good, and if you find yourself
> doing it often, the problem isn't your chart.

### D3 · The Level Card — 8 min · slide 18 · **[GATE]**

> Your Level Card. Swing high, swing low, three levels, and the date you plotted
> them.
>
> The date matters. In three months you'll look at this card and need to know
> whether it's current. Undated levels get trusted long after they should have
> been redrawn.
>
> Coaches sign every card. It's what you'll carry into Module 4 for your alarms
> and Module 6 for your ladder.
