## PART 1 · COINS.PH WALKTHROUGH — 25 min

### B1 · Funding Coins.ph — 8 min · slides 4–5 · **[LIVE]**

> Step one. Local funding account into Coins.ph.
>
> Your sources: GCash, Maya, your bank, or any other supported local method.
> Watch where I initiate the deposit.

*Demonstrate: initiate → required fields → confirm → where the balance appears.*

> Four things every single time, and they never change:
>
> Where do I start the deposit. What information does it want. How do I confirm.
> And then — **where do I verify the money actually arrived.**
>
> That last one is the one people skip. You do not move to the next step on the
> assumption that it worked. You look at the balance.
>
> **Key principle: always verify the destination and the amount before you send.**
> Say it back to me.

### B2 · Buying stablecoins — 8 min · slides 6–7 · **[LIVE]**

> Step two. Pesos into USDT or USDC.

*Demonstrate: where to buy → choose the asset → enter amount → review → confirm →
verify the resulting balance.*

> Now — why stablecoins at all? Why not just send pesos?
>
> Remember Timezone. You can't play with pesos, you convert to credits first. Same
> thing. But there's a second reason that matters more.
>
> A stablecoin is the **unit the whole strategy is denominated in.** You borrow in
> it, you earn in it, and your LTV is measured in it. It isn't "another crypto" you
> hold hoping it goes up — it's the ruler you measure everything else with. If it
> moved, none of the arithmetic from Session 1 would hold.

### B3 · Coins.ph → Binance — 9 min · slides 8–9 · **[LIVE]**

> Step three, and this is where people lose money to carelessness rather than to
> markets.

*Demonstrate the full transfer, narrating every field: withdrawal function → get
the Binance deposit address → select asset → **select network** → paste address →
check address and network → review fee → confirm → verify arrival in Binance.*

> Here is the checklist. It goes on your phone, not in your memory.
>
> **STOP BEFORE YOU SEND: ASSET → ADDRESS → NETWORK → AMOUNT → FEE → CONFIRM.**
>
> Six words. Say them with me.
>
> The one that ends people is **network**. If the asset is right and the address is
> right but the network is wrong, the money goes somewhere neither Coins.ph nor
> Binance can retrieve it from. There's no support ticket for that. It's gone.
>
> **The address and the network must match.** Both. Every time. Including the
> hundredth time, when you're confident and not looking.

*[POD] Coaches: have every participant read their six-word checklist back before
Block H.*

## PART 5 · EARN + THE BBT MECHANISM — 25 min

**The core of the session. Do not compress this to make time.**

### E1 · Asset → Collateral — 6 min · slides 17–18 · **[LIVE]**

> Now the mechanism. **Asset → Collateral → Borrow → TP.**
>
> Watch what happens when I post Bitcoin as collateral.

*Demonstrate posting collateral. Show the state before and after.*

> Nothing about your Bitcoin changed. You still own the same amount. What changed
> is that it's now **pledged** — the platform has a claim on it. Exactly like a
> title deposited with a bank, except it took four seconds instead of six weeks.

### E2 · Borrow and borrowing capacity — 6 min · slides 19–20 · **[LIVE]**

*Demonstrate the borrow screen and the maximum the platform offers.*

> Look at what it's offering. **[LIVE]** — read the max LTV out loud,
> {{max_initial_ltv}}.
>
> That is the platform telling you the most it will lend. It is **not advice.** The
> platform earns on the loan. Its interests and yours are not the same, and there
> is no rule that says the number it offers is a number you should take.
>
> Session 1, drill one: 78% LTV, a 10% drop, margin call. This is the screen where
> that decision gets made.

### E3 · LTV and adjusting it — 8 min · slides 21–22 · **[LIVE]**

*Demonstrate the LTV slider. Move it and let the room watch the numbers change.*

> Watch the slider. As I pull LTV **up**, the borrow amount goes up — and look at
> the liquidation price climbing toward today's price. As I pull it **down**, I
> borrow less, and the liquidation price falls away from me.
>
> That slider is the single most consequential control in this app.
>
> Now set it to **30%.** Not the platform's max. Not 50%. **Thirty.**
>
> Why 30 — 30% LTV survives a **67% drawdown**. Fifty survives 45%, and Bitcoin has
> fallen more than 45% three times in the last eight years. Thirty is not caution.
> It's the number that survives the actual history of this asset.

### E4 · Liquidation price on the screen — 5 min · slide 23 · **[LIVE]**

> And here it is. The platform prints your liquidation price for you.
>
> **P_liquidation = entry price × (your LTV ÷ 0.91)**
>
> At 30% LTV: entry × 0.33. If you entered at $100,000, you're liquidated around
> **$33,000.**
>
> Compare that to 78%: entry × 0.857. Liquidated at **$85,700** — a 14% dip away.
>
> Same asset. Same money. One slider.

## PART 6 · YEN CARRY TRADE PRINCIPLE — 8 min · slides 24–25

### F1 · Why we borrow at all

> Step back. **Why** are we borrowing? We're not borrowing because we need money.
>
> Session 1: borrow cheap, deploy into something that returns more. Institutions
> did it with the yen for thirty years. You're doing it with a stablecoin against
> Bitcoin.
>
> **Low-cost borrowing → capital deployed into a higher-returning position.** Same
> chain.
>
> But this is **not** a copy of the institutional trade, and I want you clear on
> the differences, because they're the parts that can hurt you.
>
> An institution borrowing yen posts no collateral that can be seized on a price
> move — their risk is the exchange rate. **You post collateral.** Your Bitcoin can
> be sold out from under you. That's a risk they don't have.
>
> And their trade still blew up. August 2024, the yen moved and the whole thing
> unwound in days. Yours has the same failure mode with an extra trapdoor.
>
> Which is why we're at 30% and not at 78%.

## PART 7 · LIQUIDATION PRICE — 15 min

### G1 · The relationship — 6 min · slide 27

> One relationship, and it runs the rest of your time in this programme.
>
> **LTV up → risk up → liquidation price moves toward today's price.**
> **LTV down → more buffer → liquidation price moves away.**
>
> Nothing else you do on that app matters as much as which direction you move that
> slider.

### G2 · What liquidation actually is — 5 min · slide 28

> And I want you to understand what the number *means*, not memorise it.
>
> Liquidation is not a penalty. It isn't the platform being unfair. It's the
> platform **protecting its loan.** Once your collateral gets close to being worth
> less than what you owe, it sells — because if it waits, it loses money.
>
> Understand that and you stop being surprised by it. The platform is not your
> partner. It's a lender with an automatic switch, and you know where the switch is.

### G3 · Everyone finds their own — 4 min · slide 29 · **[POD] [GATE]**

> Everybody, right now: find your liquidation price on your screen and **write it

>
> Then write next to it: *"If Bitcoin reaches this number, I have already lost the
> position."* In your own handwriting.
>
> Coaches — every person, every pod. Nobody moves to Part 8 without it.
