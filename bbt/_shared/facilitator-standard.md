# The Facilitator Standard

The programme is taught live, so the programme scales only as far as the number of
people who can teach it. This is the document that makes a second facilitator
possible. The facilitator track certifies against it.

---

## 1. The room mechanics (inherited from Session 1, keep them)

The existing deck's participation engine works and should not be redesigned. It
runs on four moves:

**The typed check.** Ask a closed question, ask for one word in the chat, wait,
acknowledge. *"Type Yes in the chatbox." "Type ready." "Type hassle."* It converts
a silent Zoom into a room that answers, and it gives the facilitator a live read
on how many people are still with them.

**The calculator moment.** Hand the room arithmetic they can do. *"Get your
calculators out, what is 80% of 2M?"* People who compute a number own it.

**The filter.** Ask an open question, take real answers, thank them, then steer to
the intended one. *"If they don't say 'borrow against it', filter it."* Never
correct a participant in front of the room — acknowledge, then add.

**The named callback.** *"Sino dito naglaro na sa Timezone?"* Analogies from the
room's own life — the arcade token, the Cashflow board, refinancing a title. Every
new module needs one of these and it must be local. A facilitator who cannot find
a local analogy for their own room is not ready to run the session.

Language: Taglish, as the room runs. Do not sand it into corporate English.

## 2. The three things a facilitator may never do

1. **Never present a platform number from a slide.** Rates, LTV thresholds, and
   prices are read live off the platform and spoken aloud. If the platform is
   down, say so and use the abstract worked example.
2. **Never soften the standing disclosure.** Read it as written.
3. **Never promise a return.** Not a range, not a "historically", not a
   "conservatively". Teach the mechanism and the failure mode; the room does its
   own arithmetic.

## 3. Timing discipline

Every script in this repo is beat-timed. The timings are not decoration — they are
what lets a second facilitator run the same session in the same slot.

- A beat that runs 50% over is a beat to cut, not a session to extend.
- The 5-minute break is real. Take it. The Session 1 script puts it after the
  liquidation drills for a reason: that is the heaviest module.
- Q&A is bounded and stated up front. *"Two to three questions, the rest we handle
  in the live session."* Say the bound before opening the floor.

## 4. Handling the room's hardest questions

**"How much will I make?"** — "I won't give you a number, and anyone who does is
selling you something. What I'll give you is the mechanism and the price where it
breaks. You decide what that's worth."

**"Is this safe?"** — "No. It's leveraged exposure to the most volatile major
asset there is. What we teach is how to size it so that being wrong doesn't end
you." Then the drawdown table.

**"Why not just put it all in?"** — the drawdown table, then the reserve rule.

**"My friend does 78% LTV."** — "Then a 15% drop closes him out. Bitcoin does 15%
in a bad week." Do not editorialise beyond the arithmetic.

**"What if Binance goes down?"** — "Then this doesn't protect you. That's a real
risk and it's why nothing you can't lose goes in." Never wave this away.

## 5. The pod structure (this is what makes execution sessions scale)

The execution sessions (2, and the drill halves of 3 and 4) do not scale by adding
seats. They scale by adding pods.

- **One coach per eight participants.** Below that ratio, hands-on troubleshooting
  fails and people leave with broken accounts.
- **Pre-flight gate before the room opens.** Verification, funding and app install
  are confirmed *asynchronously in the days before*, not in the session. A
  participant who arrives unverified goes to the next cohort's execution session,
  not into this room. This single rule is the difference between a 40-minute
  session and a three-hour one.
- **Coaches run the checklist, the lead runs the narrative.** The lead facilitator
  never stops to fix one person's app.
- **Screen-share order:** lead demonstrates once end to end, then pods execute,
  then lead re-demonstrates the step that most pods failed on.

## 6. What a facilitator carries into every room

- The cohort variables file, filled in that morning.
- The live platform, logged in, on a screen they can share.
- `_shared/ltv-math.md`, from memory, not from the file.
- The standing disclosure.
- A local analogy of their own for at least one module.
