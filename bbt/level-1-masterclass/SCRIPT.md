## MODULE 1 · BUY BORROW DIE — 65 min

### B1 · The land — 3 min · slides 5–6

> Year 2021. You have **1 million pesos**. Someone offers you one hectare in BGC

*Wait.* "You just bought one hectare of land. Congratulations!"

*Let them celebrate. Do not rush this.*

### B2 · The filter — 4 min · slide 7

*Take two or three real answers. Thank each one. If nobody says "borrow against

> All good answers. Here's the one we're after — you can **borrow** against it.
>
> Who here is familiar with the word "refinancing"? With using land as

### B3 · The bank walk — 12 min · slides 8–14 *(single beat, seven build slides)*

*Walk the build one letter at a time. Let each step land before advancing.*

> **A.** You go to the bank.
> **B.** You fill up forms for a loan against your property. This is an
> **asset-backed loan** — a loan against something you own that has value.
> **C.** The bank assesses whether it's really worth 2 million. You pay an
> appraisal fee.
> **D.** Then due diligence. Is the title actually yours? Are your real estate
> taxes up to date? Your other government payments?
> **E.** Then you have to get **approved**. And even approved, banks only lend up
> to **80% loan-to-value**. Why do you think they cap it at 80%?

*Take answers. Thank them. Reinforce:*

> Right — the bank makes sure it always comes out ahead. That's why only 80%.
>
> **F.** Calculators out. What's 80% of 2 million?

*Wait for the chat.* "1.6 million. Correct."

> **G.** Now you pay monthly amortisation on what you borrowed.
> **H.** And here's the question. Do you actually receive the full 1.6 million?

*Take answers.*

> Correct — **may mga bawas pa yan.** Fees. Insurance. Credit loan protection.
>
> Do you feel the hassle? The forms, the back and forth, making sure everything
> is in order? And after all that — you can still be denied. And if approved,
> you're on the hook for a monthly payment.
>
> Type **hassle** in the chatbox.

*Wait.* "Thank you."

### B4 · Foreclosure — 4 min · slide 15

> What happens if one day you can't make the monthly amortisation?
>
> The bank issues you this word. **Foreclosed.**
>
> What happens to your property? Type it in the chat.

*Take answers.* "Thank you, thank you."

> They take the asset, and they sell it. Note the timeline though — this takes

### B5 · The same shape, digitally — 5 min · slides 16–17

> Now — how do we do this with cryptocurrency?
>
> Physical real estate is land. **Digital real estate is Bitcoin.** Like land, you
> can own a fraction of it. Like land, you can borrow against it.
>
> But with digital real estate you don't fill out forms. You don't travel to a
> bank. You don't wait on government agencies. You can't be rejected. And you
> don't pay appraisal fees or insurance — so you receive the whole amount you
> borrow.
>
> Same platform to buy it, same platform to borrow against it.

**Corrections C1, C2, C3 — say this, it replaces the old comparison slide:**

> Now let me be straight with you about the trade-off, because the honest version
> is more useful than the sales version.
>
> **Ticket size** — land needs millions. This needs about {{min_capital_php}}
> pesos. Smaller money at stake.
>
> **Risk** — and here I'm going to disagree with how this is usually sold. Less
> money exposed does **not** mean less risk. Bitcoin swings far harder than BGC
> land, and remember the foreclosure timeline? Months of notices? On this platform
> there are no notices and no months. If the price hits a number, the platform
> sells your Bitcoin. **Minutes, not months.** Smaller ticket, faster and deeper
> downside.
>
> **Interest** — [LIVE] read {{usdt_borrow_apr}} off the platform. That is not
> "very low". It's roughly what a Philippine bank charges on a secured loan —
> without the consumer protections.
>
> **"You never have to sell"** — you are choosing not to sell *today*. The loan is
> still owed. You repay it from income, or you repay it from a later borrow, or
> eventually the platform sells for you. This strategy buys you **timing**. It does

### B6 · The numbers — 4 min · slide 18

> Say you have **$100**. Bitcoin is at **$100,000** — I'm using a round number so
> the math is clean; [LIVE] today it's actually {{btc_price_usd}}.
>
> $100 buys you 0.001 BTC. And the platform will let you borrow up to **78%** of
> that instantly. [LIVE] verify {{max_initial_ltv}}.
>
> Now — should you?

### B7 · Why 78% ends you — 5 min · slides 19–21

> Let's find out. You borrow at 78% LTV. Bitcoin drops 10% — from $100,000 to
> $90,000. Where's your LTV now?
>
> Two ways to get there. The quick field check: add the drop to your LTV.
> 78 plus 10 is 88%. **The platform's actual math** divides instead: 78 divided by
> 0.90 is **86.7%**.
>
> Write both down, and know the difference. The quick version always reads a
> little higher, so it errs on the safe side — but the app will show you the
> lower number and I don't want you thinking the app is lying to you.
>
> Either way, you've crossed **85%**. That's a **margin call**. The platform tells
> you: repay, or add collateral.
>
> Keep falling. At **91% LTV**, the platform stops asking. It **liquidates** —
> sells your Bitcoin to cover the loan. That's foreclosure, except it takes
> seconds and nobody sends you a letter.
>
> A 10% Bitcoin drop is a Tuesday. That's what 78% LTV buys you.

### B8 · The drills — 6 min · slides 22–25

*Run both. Do not skip the losing one.*

> **Drill one.** You bought at **$90,000**. You borrowed to **60% LTV**. Bitcoin
> drops to **$50,000**. Liquidated or not? Calculators.
>
> Step one, the drop: 90,000 minus 50,000 is 40,000.
> Step two, the percentage: 40,000 over 90,000, times 100 — **44%**.
> Step three, the new LTV: 60 divided by 0.556 — **108%**.
>
> 108 is well past 91. **Liquidated.** Your Bitcoin is gone.
>
> Does a 44% drop happen in Bitcoin? **Yes.** It has happened in 2018, in 2021, in
> 2022. Not a black swan. A cycle.
>
> **Drill two.** Same entry, $90,000. This time you borrowed to **50%**. Bitcoin
> drops to **$60,000**.
>
> The drop: 30,000 over 90,000 — **33.3%**.
> New LTV: 50 divided by 0.667 — **75%**.
>
> 75 is under 91. **Not liquidated.** Give yourselves a hand.

### B9 · The rule the old version got wrong — 4 min · slide 26

**This is the most important beat in Module 1. Do not compress it.**

> Now here's where I'm going to correct something you may have heard elsewhere —
> including from us, in earlier cohorts.
>
> The usual rule is "keep your LTV at or below 50% and you're fine." You just
> survived drill two at 50%, so it sounds right.
>
> Run the arithmetic properly. At 50% LTV, you get liquidated at a **45% drawdown.**
>
> Bitcoin's drawdowns: 2018, about **84%**. 2021, about **53%**. 2022, about
> **77%**.
>
> 50% LTV does not survive **any** of those. It survives a correction. It does not
> survive a bear market — and a bear market is exactly when your job, your business
> and your other money are also under pressure.
>
> So the BBT rule is not 50%. It's this:
>
> **Position LTV — 30% or less.** That survives a 67% drawdown.
> **Programme LTV — 20% or less.** Loan divided by your capital *plus your reserve.*
> **The reserve — for every 100 dollars you deploy, you hold 100 dollars back.**
>
> Two different numbers, and you need to be able to say both of yours. Write them
> down now.

### C3 · The trade — 6 min · slides 31–33

> Here's what they did. Borrow in yen at 0%. Convert to US dollars. Buy US Treasury
> bills yielding 4%.
>
> Calculators. Borrow **1 billion yen** at 0%. Rate is **159 yen to the dollar**.
> How many dollars?
>
> About **6,289,000**. Put that in a 4% yield — what does it earn?
>
> **$251,572.** And what interest do you owe Japan? **Zero.**
>
> The difference between what you earn and what you pay — 4% minus 0% — that gap
> is the **carry**. That's the trade.

### C4 · The half nobody teaches — 6 min · slide 34

**Correction C5. This beat did not exist in the old deck. It is the point of the

> 4% is gone — and past a point, your capital goes with it.
>
> This isn't theory. In **August 2024**, the Bank of Japan raised rates. The yen
> surged. Everyone holding this trade had to unwind at once — sell the dollar
> assets, buy back the yen — all of them, on the same day. Japanese equities fell
> double digits. Global markets fell. **Crypto fell.** Positions that had earned
> the carry for years gave it all back inside a week.
>
> The trade the textbooks call clever is the same trade that blew up.
>
> Now hold that, because here's the part that matters to you. When you run this

>

### D6 · The catch, and the reserve — 4 min · slide 47

**Correction C7. This was a speaker note in the old deck. It is now a slide.**

> Here's the catch, and it's the one that actually matters.
>
> **What if the price goes below your levels?** Below 0.382? Below where your plan
> said it would stop?
>
> **That's when the reserve comes in.** For every $100 of capital you deploy, you
> hold **$100 back.** Untouched. Not in the position. Not in Earn where you'd be

>

>

> call**, the top-up has to come from the **reserve**. Not from borrowing more.

>
