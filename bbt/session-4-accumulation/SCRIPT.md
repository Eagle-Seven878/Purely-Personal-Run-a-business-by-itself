# Session 4 · ACCUMULATION — Facilitator Script

**Format** Live · 150 minutes · pods of 8
**Entry gate** Session 3 pass — Risk Card, drill passed unaided, **reserve funded 1:1**
**Pass gate** **90-Day Accumulation Plan** with pre-committed levels, coach-signed
**Slides** [`SLIDES.md`](SLIDES.md)

This rung resolves the open conflict in the source curriculum: Session 1 Module 2
tells participants to put borrowed capital into Earn, Module 3 tells them to hold
it and buy dips, and nothing tells them how to split it. That decision is made
here, in writing, before any money moves.

---

## BLOCK A · OPEN — 5 min · slides 1–2

Read the standing disclosure. Then reserve check:

> Before anything: coaches, confirm reserve funded 1:1, pod by pod. Anyone short

> Three sessions in, here's where you are. You can open a position. You can defend
> one. Tonight is the first session that's about **growing** one — and it's the
> first session where the failure mode is greed rather than ignorance.

## MODULE 1 · THE ALLOCATION RULE — 25 min

### B1 · Naming the conflict — 6 min · slide 3

> I want to name something you've been carrying since Session 1, because some of
> you have noticed and been too polite to say it.
>
> Module 2 said: borrow USDT, convert to USDC, put it in Earn, collect the spread.
> Module 3 said: borrow USDT, hold it, buy the dip.
>
> **Those are two different jobs for the same dollar and you cannot do both with
> it.** Every dollar sitting in Earn is a dollar not available for a dip. Every
> dollar waiting for a dip is a dollar earning nothing.
>
> Tonight we split it. On paper. Before it matters.

### B2 · The two jobs — 8 min · slide 4

> **Carry capital** — sits in Earn, collects the spread, works every day, and is
> not there when the dip comes. Certain, small, continuous.
>
> **Dry powder** — sits in stablecoin, earns nothing, and is there the day price
> hits your level. Uncertain, lumpy, occasional.
>
> The mistake almost everyone makes is holding it all as carry — because carry
> feels productive and cash feels lazy — and then watching the best entry in
> eighteen months arrive with nothing to buy it with.
>
> The opposite mistake is holding it all as dry powder for a dip that takes two
> years, earning zero the whole time.

### B3 · The rule — 11 min · slides 5–6

> BBT's split, and you may vary it with your coach, not on your own:
>
> **60% carry · 40% dry powder.**
>
> And a rule that overrides the split: **dry powder is topped up before carry.**
> If a dip consumes your dry powder, the next borrowed dollar rebuilds it.
>
> Now — the line that keeps you out of trouble. **Reserve is not dry powder.**
>
> Reserve defends the position you have. Dry powder buys a position you don't have
> yet. They look identical in your account and they are opposite in purpose.
> Spending reserve on a dip is the single most common way a disciplined person
> blows up: they buy the 40% drawdown with their defence money, and the 60%
> drawdown liquidates them.
>
> **Three buckets. Say them.** Reserve — defends. Dry powder — buys. Carry — earns.
>
> Write all three amounts on your plan tonight, separately.

## MODULE 2 · THE ACCUMULATION LADDER — 30 min

### C1 · The levels — 8 min · slides 7–8

> TCIB gave you the zones: **0.618, 0.50, 0.382.** Session 1 said we accumulate
> across them instead of selling into them.
>
> Tonight they become a **pre-committed ladder** — an instruction you write now and
> obey later, specifically so that the person deciding is you-today rather than
> you-in-a-panic.

### C2 · Laddering the dry powder — 12 min · slides 9–10

> Do not spend dry powder at one level. You will always be early or always be late.
> Ladder it:
>
> **0.618 — deploy 30%** of dry powder. Shallowest, most likely to be reached,
> smallest commitment.
> **0.50 — deploy 30%.**
> **0.382 — deploy 40%.** Deepest, least likely, biggest reward for reaching it.
>
> Weighted to the deeper levels on purpose. If price only reaches 0.618 you've
> deployed 30% and kept 70% for better. If it goes all the way, you deployed most
> of it near the bottom.
>
> Worked. $40 dry powder, Fib high $120,000, low $80,000:
> 0.618 → $104,720 — deploy **$12**
> 0.50 → $100,000 — deploy **$12**
> 0.382 → $95,280 — deploy **$16**
>
> Compute yours now with tonight's levels. Coaches, check the arithmetic.

### C2b · Where the bought Bitcoin goes — 4 min · slide 10b

> One operational rule, and it keeps every number in your plan stable.
>
> **Bitcoin you buy with dry powder is held in Spot. It does not go into
> collateral.**
>
> Here's why. If you keep adding your accumulated Bitcoin to collateral, your
> liquidation price moves every time you buy, your four alarms go stale, and the
> plan you wrote stops describing the position you have.
>
> Leave it in Spot and your loan stays $30, your liquidation price stays where you
> wrote it, and your alarms stay true. The accumulated Bitcoin is the **point** of
> the exercise — it isn't working capital.
>
> You re-base deliberately once a year, at the annual review, with your coach. Not
> on a Tuesday because a chart looked good.

### C3 · Below the ladder — 10 min · slide 11

> And what if price goes below 0.382? Below every level you planned for?
>
> First, know the honest answer: **it happens, and it happens in the years that
> matter most.** 2018 and 2022 both went far below any reasonable ladder.
>
> Second, know what you do: **nothing with dry powder — it's spent. Nothing with
> reserve — that's not what it's for.**
>
> What you do is check your LTV, follow the Session 3 decision tree, and if the
> alarms fire, defend. Your job below the ladder is not to catch the bottom. It's
> to still own your Bitcoin when the bottom is behind you.
>
> New capital from your income can buy down there. Borrowed capital and reserve
> cannot.

## MODULE 3 · BORROW UP, BUY DOWN — 25 min

### D1 · The pattern — 8 min · slide 12

> Session 1 showed the shape: price up, you borrow, not sell. Price down, you buy
> with what you borrowed.
>
> Now the constraint that keeps it safe. **Borrowing on the way up must not raise
> your LTV above 30%.**
>
> Here's the thing people miss and it's good news: when price rises, your
> collateral is worth more, so your LTV **falls on its own**. That fall is your
> borrowing capacity — and it's the only borrowing capacity you're allowed to use.

### D2 · Worked — 10 min · slides 13–14

> Entry $100,000, $100 deployed, $30 borrowed. LTV 30%.
>
> Price rises to **$130,000.** Collateral now $130. Your LTV without touching
> anything: 30 ÷ 130 = **23%.**
>
> To return to 30% you can borrow up to $39 total, so **$9 more.** That $9 is
> yours, and it splits 60/40 like everything else: $5.40 carry, $3.60 dry powder.
>
> Note how small that is. **Rising prices do not hand you much.** Anyone telling
> you leverage compounds fast on the way up is describing a much higher LTV than
> you run, and describing a much shorter holding period than you have.

### D3 · What never happens — 7 min · slide 15

> Three things that never happen in this programme, no matter what the chart is
> doing:
>
> **One.** LTV above 30% to buy a dip. The dip is why you have dry powder. If dry
> powder is gone, the dip is not yours.
>
> **Two.** Reserve deployed into a dip. Ever.
>
> **Three.** Buying more collateral with borrowed money during a margin call. It
> raises the loan and the collateral together. It does not rescue anything and it
> makes the eventual liquidation bigger.
>
> If you find yourself reasoning toward any of these at 2am, the reasoning is the
> symptom. Message your coach instead.

## MODULE 4 · SCENARIO LAB — 40 min · **[POD]**

*Six drills. Pods work them, coaches check arithmetic and — more importantly — the
reasoning. Baseline for all six: entry $100,000 · $100 deployed · $30 borrowed ·
LTV 30% · reserve $100 · dry powder $12 · carry $18.*

**Drill 1 · Shallow dip — slide 17**
Price → $104,720 (0.618). *Deploy $12? No — that's your full dry powder at the
shallowest level. Deploy 30% of dry powder = **$3.60**.* Tests the ladder.

**Drill 2 · Straight to the bottom — slide 18**
Price → $95,280 (0.382) without pausing. *All three rungs triggered on the way
through. Deploy the full $12 — but note you got the whole ladder at a better
average than if it had stepped down.* Tests reading the ladder as levels, not
events.

**Drill 3 · The tempting one — slide 19**
Price → $70,000. Dry powder spent. Reserve $100, untouched. *LTV = 30 ÷ 0.70 =
**42.9%**. Alarm 1 has fired. **Deploy nothing.** Log it.* Tests whether reserve
holds under temptation. **Coaches: watch for the pod that reasons its way into
"just a little".**

**Drill 4 · Up and out — slide 20**
Price → $150,000. *LTV = 20%. Capacity to 30% = $45 total, so borrow **$15** more.
Split $9 carry / $6 dry powder. Then note: your liquidation price has moved to
$150,000 × (0.30 ÷ 0.91) = **$49,451** — higher than before. Recompute all four alarms.*
Tests that borrowing more moves the liquidation price **up**, which is the cost
people forget.

**Drill 5 · The spread inverts — slide 21**
Borrow rate rises above the earn rate. *Carry is now costing money. Action: move
carry capital to dry powder or repay part of the loan. Do not hold a negative
carry because the strategy has a name.* Tests Session 5's material early.

**Drill 6 · Everything at once — slide 22**
Price → $60,000, spread inverted, reserve $100 intact. *LTV = 50%. Alarm 2:
calculate, don't execute — top-up to 30% needs collateral $100, have $60, so $40
computed and held ready. Spread inverted so repay from carry capital instead —
repaying **reduces the loan**, which fixes the LTV and the negative carry at
once.* Tests integration. This is the drill that separates the ready from the
almost-ready.

## MODULE 5 · WRITE THE PLAN — 15 min · slide 23 · **[GATE]**

> Everything tonight goes onto one page, and the page is your pass.
>
> **Your three buckets** — reserve, dry powder, carry. Amounts.
> **Your ladder** — three price levels, three deploy amounts, computed tonight.
> **Your borrow-up rule** — the LTV that permits a borrow, and the 60/40 split.
> **Your never-list** — the three things from Module 3, in your handwriting.
> **Your review date** — a calendar entry, 90 days out, made in this room.
>
> The point of a written plan is not that it's clever. It's that it was written by
> someone calm, and it will be read by someone who isn't.
>
> Coaches sign every plan before anyone leaves.
