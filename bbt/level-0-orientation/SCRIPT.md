## BLOCK B · THE LAND — 25 min · **[LAYER A]**

### B1 · You bought the land — 4 min · slides 4–5

> It's 2021. You have **1 million pesos**. Someone offers you a hectare in BGC at
> 30% below market. Do you buy it?
>
> Type **Yes**.

*"You just bought a hectare in BGC. Congratulations."*

> Five years pass. That land is now worth **2 million pesos.**

*Let them celebrate.*

### B2 · What do you do with it — 5 min · slide 6

> Now. You want to get money out of that land. What are your options?
> Raise your hand.

*Take two or three. Thank each one. Filter to:*

> Right. **You can borrow against it.** Who here knows the word *refinancing*?
> Who has used property as *collateral*? Thank you.
>
> And notice what you did **not** have to do. You didn't have to sell it.

### B3 · The bank walk — 10 min · slides 7–12

*Walk the build. Let each step land.*

> You go to the bank. You fill up forms. They appraise it — **you** pay for that.
> Then due diligence: is the title really yours, are your real estate taxes
> current. Then approval, which you might not get.
>
> And even approved, the bank lends you only **80%** of value. Calculators —
> what's 80% of 2 million?

*"1.6 million. Correct."*

> Do you receive 1.6 million? **May mga bawas pa yan.** Fees. Insurance. Credit
> loan protection.
>
> Feel the hassle? Type **hassle** in the chat.
>
> But here's the part that matters tonight. Through all of that — **you never
> sold the land.** You still own the hectare. You got liquidity out of an asset
> you kept.

### B4 · The only way you lose it — 6 min · slide 13 · **[LAYER A]**

**This is the beat the whole programme rests on. Do not rush it.**

> One question. In that whole arrangement — what is the only thing that takes the
> land away from you?

*Take answers. Steer to it.*

> **Foreclosure.** You stop paying, the bank issues the notice, and eventually
> they sell your land.
>
> So follow this. You didn't lose the land because the market dropped. You didn't
> lose it because you were wrong about BGC. You lost it because you were
> **forced to sell at a moment you didn't choose.**
>
> Write this down, because it's the sentence the rest of the programme is built
> on:
>
> **A drop is not a loss until you are forced to sell.**
>
> Everything we teach is about removing the force.

## BLOCK D · CAPITAL PRESERVATION — 20 min · **[LAYER A]**

### D1 · The seven years — 8 min · slide 17

> Let me show you what this asset actually does, because nobody selling you

*Walk [`../_shared/capital-preservation.md`](../_shared/capital-preservation.md)

> Roughly once a year, for seven straight years, Bitcoin has fallen hard.
> Half. And in 2022, **seventy-seven percent.**
>
> Anyone who tells you that's unusual is lying or hasn't looked. It's the
> weather.

### D2 · So how is anyone still holding — 8 min · slide 18

> So how does anybody come through that still owning their Bitcoin?
>
> Not by predicting it. Nobody predicted it.
>
> They come through it by **borrowing so little that the forced sale never
> reaches them** — and by writing down, in advance, what they'll do when the
> price falls. That's it. That's the whole trick, and it's boring, which is why
> it works and why it doesn't sell well.
>
> We teach a ceiling on how much you borrow, a reserve you never touch, and four
> alarms with a written instruction attached to each one.
>
> Run those rules through every drawdown of the last seven years and the position
> is **never force-sold.** Not once.

### D3 · The honest half — 4 min · slide 19 · **[LAYER A]**

**Never skip this beat. It is the credibility of the whole programme.**

> And now the honest half, because I'd rather you hear it from me tonight than

> about 23 cents on the peso. Our rules did not stop that and nothing can.
>
> What our rules did was make sure you **still owned all of it** — so that when
> it recovered, it recovered in your hands and not somebody else's.
>
> That's the difference we teach:
> **preserve the asset, and you get to choose your own timing.**
>
