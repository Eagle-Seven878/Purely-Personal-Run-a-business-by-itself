Level 4 is not a class. It is a year of faculty working beside a participant as
they apply their own playbook to their own portfolio decisions. There is no
curriculum to deliver — there is a **cadence to hold** and a set of conversations
that must happen.

The material below is the teaching content Level 4 draws on. Deliver it as it
becomes relevant to that participant's position, not as a sequence.

reachable on the day it matters. A Level 4 participant whose Alarm 3 fired and who
did not get a same-day call has not received what they bought.

## MODULE 1 · MANAGING THE SPREAD — 25 min

### B1 · The monthly check — 8 min · slides 2–3

> Once a month, four numbers. Put it in your calendar tonight.
>
> **One** — your borrow rate today.
> **Two** — your earn rate today, **blended**, not the headline.
> **Three** — the spread. Two minus one.
> **Four** — your position LTV.
>
> Blended matters. Flexible Earn advertises a high rate on the first small tranche
> and a much lower one above it. If you have $500 in there and the headline applies
> to the first $100, your real rate is nowhere near the poster.
>
> Work out your blended rate once, properly, and you'll never be fooled by the
> headline again.

### B2 · When it inverts — 10 min · slides 4–5

> Both rates float independently. **The spread can go negative.** When it does you
> are paying to run the strategy, which is not the strategy.
>
> Three responses, in order of preference:
>
> **One — repay part of the loan.** Reduces the loan, reduces the LTV, ends the
> negative carry. Best answer almost always, and the one people resist because it
> feels like retreating.
>
> **Two — move carry capital to dry powder.** Stops the bleed, keeps the optionality
> if you think the inversion is temporary.
>
> **Three — do nothing, for a stated period.** Only legitimate if you write down
> *how long* and *what you'll do at the end*. "I'll wait and see" is not a plan;
> "I'll review in 30 days and repay if it's still negative" is.
>
> What you never do is hold a negative carry indefinitely because the strategy has
> a name. **The mechanism serves you. You don't serve the mechanism.**

### B3 · The honest arithmetic — 7 min · slide 6

> Let's size what the carry actually earns, because expectations are where people
> get hurt.
>
> $30 borrowed, spread 2% — that's **60 cents a year.** Sixty cents.
>
> That is not the return. The carry is not what makes this worth doing.
>
> What makes it worth doing is **the Bitcoin you accumulated without selling any
> of it, and without adding capital you didn't have.** The carry pays for the
> transaction costs and a bit of the interest. It is a rounding error against the
> asset.
>
> Anyone who sells you this strategy on the yield is selling you the wrong thing —
> and if that's what you came for, tonight is a good night to find out.

## MODULE 2 · THE CADENCE — 20 min

### C1 · Four rhythms — 10 min · slides 7–8

> **Daily — 30 seconds.** Look at LTV. That's it. Don't open the chart, don't read
> anything. If no alarm has fired, close the app.
>
> **Monthly — 15 minutes.** The four numbers. Rates, spread, LTV. Log them.
>
> **Quarterly — 1 hour.** Re-plot your Fibonacci levels. Rebalance the three
> buckets. Recompute every alarm. Confirm reserve is still 1:1 — it drifts as the
> position grows and nobody notices.
>
> **Annually — half a day.** Re-run the whole plan. Position sizing. Estate check.
> Tax position.

### C2 · Why the daily check is deliberately boring — 5 min · slide 9

> The daily check is thirty seconds on purpose. The failure mode of this strategy
> is not neglect — it's **over-engagement.** People who watch the chart all day
> start managing the position, and managing a position you were supposed to hold
> is how you end up with less Bitcoin than you started with.
>
> Look at the number. Close the app.

### C3 · The log — 5 min · slide 10

> Keep a log. One line a month: date, price, LTV, borrow rate, earn rate, spread,
> action taken.
>
> Two reasons. **One**, in a drawdown you can see you've been here before, and that
> is worth more than any reassurance a coach can give you. **Two**, the BIR does
> not accept "I think I bought around then". Your tax position is yours, and a log
> is what makes it answerable.

## MODULE 3 · SIZING AS YOU GROW — 20 min

### D1 · Percentages, not pesos — 8 min · slide 11

> As capital grows, the rules stay the same but the pesos get big enough to
> frighten you. So the rules are written in percentages, and the percentages do
> not change:
>
> 30% position LTV at $100. 30% at $100,000.
> 1:1 reserve at $100. 1:1 at $100,000.
> 60/40 at every size.
>
> A ₱50,000 top-up is not harder than a ₱500 top-up. It's the same decision with
> more zeroes, and the plan you wrote at $100 is the plan.

### D2 · What changes with size — 7 min · slide 12

> Three things do change.
>
> **Platform concentration.** At $200, everything on one platform is fine. At
> $20,000, everything on one platform is a single point of failure, and platform
> failure is the risk no LTV rule touches. Split across platforms, or hold part
> in self-custody without a loan against it.
>
> **Tax.** At small size it's simple. At size, get an accountant who has actually
> handled crypto in the Philippines, not one who will improvise.
>
> **Your own psychology.** The number that makes you check the app at midnight is
> personal and it is real. If your position size is costing you sleep, it is too
> big — regardless of what the LTV says. That's not a soft observation; a tired
> person makes the decision that ends the position.

### D3 · When to stop growing — 5 min · slide 13

> There's a size at which you should stop adding, and almost nobody names it.
>
> Stop when this position becoming worthless would change your life. Not annoy
> you — **change it.** That's the line, and it's different for everyone, and you
> should know yours before the market makes you find out.

## MODULE 4 · THE THIRD WORD — 25 min

### E1 · What "Die" actually means — 8 min · slides 15–16

> Back to the original principle. Buy, Borrow, **Die.**
>
> The classical version: you buy an asset, you borrow against it instead of
> selling, and when you die the asset passes to your heirs. You never sold, so you
> never triggered the sale — and the heirs inherit at the value on that day.
>
> That's the mechanic. It only completes if the asset **transfers.**
>
> Now here's the part that makes this urgent rather than theoretical. Physical land
> transfers whether or not you planned for it. There's a title, there's a registry,
> there are lawyers, and the state knows the asset exists.
>
> **Bitcoin on an exchange, secured by your credentials, transfers to nobody.**
>
> If your family cannot access that account, the strategy does not complete. It
> just ends. Everything you accumulated over ten years becomes a number nobody can
> reach.

### E2 · What "Thrive" means instead — 6 min · slide 17

> We call it Thrive because there's a version that doesn't require you to die.
>
> The asset stays. The borrowing capacity grows as it appreciates. At some point
> the position can fund things — a business, a house deposit, an education —
> without being sold. That's thriving on an asset rather than consuming it.
>
> Same mechanic. Better ending. But it still depends on the transfer question,
> because a strategy measured in decades has to survive you.

### E3 · The four things — 8 min · slide 18

> Four things. Do them this quarter, not "eventually."
>
> **One — an access document.** Where the accounts are, which platforms, what the
> recovery process is. Not passwords in a text file. A written record of *what
> exists and how it is reached.*
>
> **Two — one person who knows it exists.** A strategy nobody knows about is a
> strategy that dies with you. One trusted person, minimum.
>
> **Three — the loan is a liability.** If you die with a borrow open, the platform
> still holds the claim. Your heirs inherit collateral **and** a loan, and if
> nobody services it, it liquidates. Write down what should happen to the loan.
>
> **Four — talk to someone who actually knows.** Philippine estate treatment of
> crypto is genuinely unsettled and I'm a facilitator, not your lawyer. Get proper
> advice. What I can tell you is that doing nothing is a decision, and it's the
> one that loses everything.

### E4 · The uncomfortable question — 3 min · slide 19

> Question, and I want you to actually sit with it rather than answer it quickly.
>
> **If you died tonight, could your family reach this?**
>
> If the answer is no, you don't have a strategy. You have a hobby with money in it.

>
> And one last thing. You now run something that, done properly, is boring for
> years at a time. **That's the sign it's working.** The exciting version of this
