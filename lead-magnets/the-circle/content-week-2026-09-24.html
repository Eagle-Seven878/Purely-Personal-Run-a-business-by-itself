NUMBERS

Magnet 10-Minute Money Check

Carousel · 10 slides

  1. 1

    Title

    Are you still running on feeling?

    Content

    "im basing it on feeling which i know is wrong." A café owner in Bulacan wrote that about the business, in public. The post drew 103 upvotes on a Filipino investing forum.

    Visual idea

    The quote handwritten on a torn café receipt, pinned to a counter beside a half-finished coffee.

  2. 2

    Title

    A full café can still lose money

    Content

    A busy day feels like a good day, even when it loses money. Feeling measures how tired you are, not what the business kept. So you push hardest on the exact days that cost you.

    Visual idea

    A crowded counter at noon, with a small red minus sign above the open cash drawer.

  3. 3

    Title

    Nobody tells you what guessing costs

    Content

    You restock, pay staff, pay rent, and whatever is left becomes your pay. Some months that is something, and some months it is nothing. You cannot fix a leak you have never once measured.

    Visual idea

    A peso coin jar with a crack near the base, coins slipping onto the table.

  4. 4

    Title

    First, give the business its own account

    Content

    When business cash and personal cash share one account, no number stays honest. Open a separate account for the business before you calculate anything at all. Now every peso that leaves has an owner you can name.

    Visual idea

    Two phone screens side by side, two e-wallets labelled BUSINESS and ME. No brand logos.

  5. 5

    Title

    Number one: your monthly breakeven in pesos

    Content

    Add every fixed cost the business pays each month, including your own salary. Divide by the share of each sale left after its direct costs. Below that line you are losing, and above it you are keeping.

    Visual idea

    A sales chart with one horizontal line labelled BREAKEVEN, red below it and green above.

  6. 6

    Title

    Number two: net income, on one page

    Content

    Build a one-page P&L showing sales, costs, and what the business actually kept. Update it every week, not at year end when the accountant asks. Net income stops being a mystery once you read it weekly.

    Visual idea

    One printed page on a clipboard with three highlighted rows: SALES, COSTS, NET INCOME.

  7. 7

    Title

    Number three: cash on hand, every morning

    Content

    Before you open, write down cash in, cash out, and cash on hand. Ten minutes, one notebook, the same three lines every single day. That habit, not a bigger month, is what replaces the feeling.

    Visual idea

    A kitchen timer at 10:00 beside a notebook with three handwritten lines: IN / OUT / ON HAND.

  8. 8

    Title

    ₱200K net income in the first quarter

    Content

    When I started ARB Pharmaceutical, it made ₱200K in net income in its first three months. Net income is the number that tells you whether the feeling was right. You only see it once breakeven and the weekly page exist.

    Visual idea

    A plain card: ARB · FIRST 3 MONTHS · ₱200K NET INCOME, with a small calendar showing three months ticked.

  9. 9

    Title

    Phase 1 of The Circle has a refund

    Content

    Phase 1 of The Circle is called No More Guessing, and it runs two months. By the end of month two, we show you your breakeven and your running profit account. If we cannot, you get every peso of your fee back.

    Visual idea

    A three-step staircase with the first step lit and labelled NO MORE GUESSING, and a small tag on it: REFUND.

  10. 10

    Title

    Trade the feeling for three numbers

    Content

    Breakeven, weekly net income, and cash on hand are where you start. Comment "NUMBERS" and I will send you my 10-Minute Money Check. Use it tomorrow morning, before you open the doors or the laptop.

    Visual idea

    The same torn receipt from slide 1, now with three numbers written over the old quote.

BREAKEVEN

Magnet Breakeven Sheet (with the owner-salary line built in)

Carousel · 10 slides

  1. 1

    Title

    Don't call it breakeven until you're paid

    Content

    "Nakakapanghina kasi feeling namin kumikita lang negosyo for its own expenses. Walang natitira for us." A business owner wrote that after two years of running the business. The business pays everyone on the list except the people who own it.

    Visual idea

    A shop counter at closing, the cash drawer open and empty except for receipts.

  2. 2

    Title

    Paying its own bills is not profit

    Content

    A business that covers rent, stock, and staff has reached the starting line. If you are not on the expense list, it is not profitable yet. It is a job that pays every person except the owner.

    Visual idea

    A payroll sheet with every staff name ticked and the owner's line at the bottom left blank.

  3. 3

    Title

    The missing line costs you twice

    Content

    First, you work the whole month and take home nothing at all. Then the books say break even, so nobody treats it as a problem. Two years can pass inside that single missing line.

    Visual idea

    A wall calendar, each month stamped BREAK EVEN in grey, with the owner's pay box empty on every page.

  4. 4

    Title

    Put your own salary in first

    Content

    Before any other calculation, write a fixed monthly salary for yourself as a cost. Pick an amount the business must carry, not whatever happens to be left. Now the breakeven includes the one person it usually forgets.

    Visual idea

    An expense list whose first row, in bold red ink, reads OWNER SALARY.

  5. 5

    Title

    Then find your real breakeven number

    Content

    Add every fixed cost, including that salary, into one monthly total. Divide it by the share of each sale left after direct costs. That result is the sales floor you must clear every single month.

    Visual idea

    A calculator with a sticky note above it: FIXED COSTS (WITH MY SALARY) ÷ WHAT EACH SALE LEAVES.

  6. 6

    Title

    Then see which products actually pay you

    Content

    Rank everything you sell by the net profit it leaves, not by sales. A few items usually carry the business while others eat the margin. Reprice or cut the bottom three before you try to sell harder.

    Visual idea

    A product shelf with the top three tagged green and the bottom three tagged red.

  7. 7

    Title

    Then move twenty percent before any bill

    Content

    Open a separate profit account and move 20% of every deposit into it. Do it the day the money lands, before a single bill is paid. Profit stops being leftovers once it leaves the account first.

    Visual idea

    A deposit slip with an arrow splitting off one fifth into a jar labelled PROFIT.

  8. 8

    Title

    ₱1M to ₱6.2M net income, every month

    Content

    One e-commerce client went from ₱1M a month in net income to ₱6.2M a month. It took six months, and it is net income, not sales. Revenue is what a business collects; net income is what it keeps.

    Visual idea

    A net income card: ₱1M/MONTH crossed out, ₱6.2M/MONTH written beside it, labelled 6 MONTHS.

  9. 9

    Title

    My own company, first three months

    Content

    When I started ARB Pharmaceutical, it made ₱200K in net income in its first three months. Both results are measured on one line only, and it is net income. Your salary belongs above that line, as a cost, every month.

    Visual idea

    A plain card: ARB · FIRST 3 MONTHS · ₱200K NET INCOME.

  10. 10

    Title

    Your breakeven must include your pay

    Content

    Without your salary, you calculated the business's breakeven, not yours. Comment "BREAKEVEN" and I will send you my Breakeven Sheet, salary line included. Fill it in this weekend, before Monday's first deposit lands.

    Visual idea

    The empty cash drawer from slide 1, now holding one envelope labelled MY SALARY.
