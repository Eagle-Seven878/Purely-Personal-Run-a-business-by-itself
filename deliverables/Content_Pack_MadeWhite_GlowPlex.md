### Post 2: The honest read on MadeWhite
I read the full MadeWhite deck so you don't have to. Three things worth knowing.

**What's solid:**
Lab work shows it lowers COX-2 (-68%) and PAR-2 (-75%) in stressed skin cells, the inflammation route to pigment. The safety file is deep: irritation, a 50-person repeat-insult patch test, mutagenicity, eye irritation.

**What's modest:**
In the 23-woman 8-week study, the melanin index fell ~27% on the treated patch, but ~23% on the untreated control. Real gap: about 4-5 points.

**What to watch:**
In a lotion at 45°C with light exposure, activity dropped to roughly 55-60% at six months. Packaging matters.

Good fit for post-acne or shaving marks. Not a magic bullet.

Ingredient decks reward careful reading. Always check the control group.

---

### Post 3: The honest read on GlowPlex
Fision® GlowPlex is built around the glass-skin trend. Here's what its clinical data actually shows.

18 women, half-face, placebo-controlled, 5% in a cream, 28 days:

• Radiance: +6% vs +2% placebo
• Dark-spot lightness (ITA°): +51% vs +28% placebo
• 89% felt glowier skin

Impressive direction. But:
→ n=18, one skin type and one population
→ placebo also improved noticeably
→ the evenness result vs placebo is only p<0.1
→ the "36% less melanin" headline is a cell assay at 0.1%; 3D skin showed 21%

Promising, and still early. I'd run it in your own base next to a niacinamide-only control.

### Post 4: Decision framework
Choosing a brightening active? I use two questions.

**1. What caused the pigment?**
Inflammation (acne, shaving, irritation) → look at anti-inflammatory routes like madecassoside.
Dullness and uneven tone → look at multi-target blends like niacinamide + peptides.

**2. What proof do you have in hand?**
Safety dossier, stability data, placebo-controlled results: check which are actually in the file, not just the headline.

Save this before your next ingredient meeting.

---

### Post 5: Formulator tip
Quick formulator note on two water-soluble brighteners.

MadeWhite™: soluble in water, glycols, ethanol and PEG-60/polysorbate-20 systems. Insoluble in squalane and macadamia oil. Water phase only. As supplied pH is 3-6. Protect emulsions from heat plus light.

Fision® GlowPlex: use 1-5%, recommended pH 4-8, and all five sample formulas add it later in the process. Confirm the processing temperature with the supplier.

Small details like these decide whether a lab claim survives in a real jar.

## 6. Claim-safety cheat sheet

| Safe to say | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "In a supplier-run study of 23 women, the treated patch showed a significant melanin-index reduction vs control at weeks 6 and 8" | "Clinically proven to whiten skin" |
| "In an 18-woman placebo-controlled study, dark-spot lightness improved 51% vs 28% for placebo at 28 days" | "2× better than any niacinamide" (no niacinamide-alone clinical arm) |
| "Lab data show COX-2 and PAR-2 reduction in stressed keratinocytes" | "Treats hyperpigmentation" (disease-style claim) |
| "Reduced melanin by 21% in a 3D Asian skin model after 15 days" | "Reduces melanin by 36% in skin" (that figure is a 0.1% cell assay) |
