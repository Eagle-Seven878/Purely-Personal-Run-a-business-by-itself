# Brand and packaging brief: premium global brightening line

**Scope:** two products from the formulation proposal: a Daily Glow Cream (niacinamide-peptide active) and a Dark-Mark Serum (madecassoside active), made by Midori Naturals Cosmetics, Inc.
**Status:** recommendations. Every name below is a working name. None has been checked for trademark, domain or social-handle availability. Do that before you commit (section 6).

## 1. Positioning

**Recommended: "clinical botanical radiance."** Botanical origin (gotu kola, quinoa, pea) plus measurable results, priced and presented as premium.

Why it fits both actives and travels globally:
- The evidence is for brightening, glow, even tone and dark-mark appearance, not for "whitening". "Whitening" is also a sensitive word in many markets, and the trade is moving to "brightening".
- Glow, glass skin and niacinamide are mainstream demand in the supplier market data.
- A two-product line with one clear job each ("Glow" and "Mark") is easy to explain in any language.

Avoid in the name and on pack: "white", "whitening", "bleach", "fair", "lightening" in the brand itself. Keep claims as appearance claims ("visibly brighter", "even-looking tone").

## 2. Name criteria (what "strongest global" means in practice)

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Short | 2-3 syllables, 5-8 letters |
| Pronounceable | Say it aloud in English, Spanish, Arabic, Mandarin, Japanese. No hard clusters |
| No bad meanings | Native-speaker check in each launch market, including slang |
| Ownable | Coined or modified word, not a dictionary word, so it can be trademarked widely |
| Visual | Looks good in a logo, on a bottle, as a social handle |
| Domain | .com available or a defensible alternative |

## 3. Name candidates (working names only)

| Name | Idea | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aurevé** | "aura" + "rêve" (dream) | Soft, luxurious, easy to say; suits glow | Likely close to existing marks; accent mark complicates handles |
| **Verdalune** | verdant (Midori = green) + lune (moon-glow) | Ties to Midori's green heritage and radiance; very ownable | Longer; "lune" reads French |
| **Soleré** | sol (sun) + a Romance ending | Radiance; global-friendly sound | Sun imagery can clash with sun-care claims; likely crowded |
| **Midori Lumen** | house-name extension | Instant link to the manufacturer's trust | Not independent of Midori; limits future manufacturer change |

**My pick for testing first: Verdalune.** It is the most ownable, it carries the green/botanical story and the glow story in one word, and a coined compound is easier to register than a dictionary word. Aurevé is the runner-up if you want something shorter.

## 4. Visual identity

- **Palette:** deep forest green (botanical, Midori link), warm pearl ivory, soft champagne-gold accent. This avoids the common pink/purple "glow" look and signals premium and natural.
- **Logo:** lowercase or small-caps wordmark in a refined serif; one simple leaf-and-sun/moon mark for embossing and the cap.
- **Voice:** calm, precise, evidence-led. Show the number, not the hype.
- **Product names:** keep the job in the name.
  - "Glow Cream" (niacinamide-peptide active)
  - "Mark Serum" or "Even Serum" (madecassoside active)

## 5. Packaging

Premium also has to be functional. The MadeWhite data shows activity loss under heat plus light, and niacinamide/peptide creams benefit from limiting air exposure.

| | Dark-Mark Serum | Daily Glow Cream |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 30 ml | 50 ml |
| Primary pack | **Opaque or deeply tinted airless pump** (frosted glass look in PP/PET) | **Airless pump or jar with inner pot**, refillable |
| Why | Blocks light and air; no dropper exposure | Hygiene, dose control, refill story |
| Finish | Matte deep green, champagne-gold collar, minimal print | Matte ivory body, gold collar |
| Secondary | FSC-certified rigid carton, uncoated stock, foil on the wordmark | Same system so the pair looks like a set |
| Sustainability | Post-consumer recycled content where available; mono-material parts; refill option | Refill pods or inner pots (a premium and sustainability signal) |
| Label content | INCI list, batch and manufacture date, period-after-opening (PAO) symbol, net content, usage instructions, manufacturer/importer address, multilingual panel for each market | Same |

**Notes**
- Glass looks most premium but adds weight, breakage and shipping cost. A heavy-feel plastic airless gives most of the look with better protection.
- The decorating details (foil, emboss, soft-touch) are where cost climbs; spend them on the carton and cap, not the whole bottle.
- Every launch market has its own labeling and claim rules (EU, UK, US, ASEAN, China, GCC). Check them per market before artwork is final.
- Ask Midori what packaging they can source or fill (airless lines need compatible filling equipment) and the minimum order quantities, before finalizing the design.

## 6. Before you lock the brand

1. **Trademark search and filing:** WIPO Global Brand Database, USPTO, EUIPO, and the markets that matter (China needs a Chinese-character brand and a pre-emptive filing; Japan and Korea too). Class 3 (cosmetics); consider class 35 (retail). Use a trademark attorney.
2. **Domain and social handles:** check .com, the main country domains and the handles on the platforms you will sell on.
3. **Linguistic check:** native speakers per launch market.
4. **Packaging dummies:** order small samples of the airless pump and carton; test fill, stability and drop/leak.
5. **Regulatory:** claims, labeling and ingredient status per market (EU/UK require a responsible person and a cosmetic product safety report).

## 7. Open questions that change the recommendation

- Which markets first, and what price tier (prestige, masstige)?
- Do you want an independent brand or a Midori-endorsed one?
- Is a natural or organic certification planned? It affects ingredients and packaging.
- Budget range and target order quantity for packaging.
